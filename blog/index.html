Top 10 French Idioms for Everyday Conversations

French Idioms Vocabulary List B1-B2

Idioms give your language natural flavor and help you sound like a native speaker. Here are 10 authentic French idioms with literal meanings, figurative translations, and realistic conversational examples.

1. Poser un lapin (Lit: To place a rabbit)
Meaning: To stand someone up / to fail to meet someone for a date or meeting.
"Il ne vient pas ? Il m'a encore posé un lapin !" (He's not coming? He stood me up again!)
2. Avoir le cafard (Lit: To have the cockroach)
Meaning: To feel blue / to be feeling down or depressed.
"Le temps est si gris aujourd'hui, j'ai un peu le cafard." (The weather is so gray today, I'm feeling a bit blue.)
3. Coûter les yeux de la tête (Lit: To cost the eyes of the head)
Meaning: To cost an arm and a leg / to be extremely expensive.
"Cet appartement à Paris coûte les yeux de la tête !" (This apartment in Paris costs an arm and a leg!)
4. Coup de foudre (Lit: Lightning strike)
Meaning: Love at first sight / instant attraction.
"Quand ils se sont rencontrés, ça a été le coup de foudre." (When they met, it was love at first sight.)
5. Appeler un chat un chat (Lit: To call a cat a cat)
Meaning: To call a spade a spade / to speak plainly and directly.
"Soyons honnêtes et appelons un chat un chat." (Let's be honest and call a spade a spade.)

Top 100 English Phrasal Verbs: Part 1 (Work & Communication)

English Phrasal Verbs Professional A2-B2

Phrasal verbs are essential for natural spoken English. Master these key professional phrasal verbs for workplace meetings and everyday conversations.

1. Bring up (Separable verb)
Meaning: To introduce a topic or issue into a discussion.
"She brought up the budget issue during our team meeting."
2. Call off (Separable verb)
Meaning: To cancel a planned event or meeting.
"They had to call off the afternoon conference due to technical errors."
3. Come across (Inseparable verb)
Meaning: To find or encounter something by chance.
"I came across a very useful grammar manual on COSYlanguages."
