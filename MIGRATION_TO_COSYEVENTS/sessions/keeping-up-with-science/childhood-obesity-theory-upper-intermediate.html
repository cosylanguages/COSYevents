📖 Session Vocabulary

Theory ≠ fact
a formal idea or set of ideas intended to explain something.
Scientists are re-examining a 42-year-old childhood obesity theory.
Metabolism ≠ stagnation
the chemical processes that occur within a living organism in order to maintain life.
Basal metabolism accounts for most daily energy expenditure.
Hypothesis ≠ certainty
a proposed explanation made on the basis of limited evidence.
The initial hypothesis was disproved by modern metabolic tracking.
Expenditure ≠ intake
the action of spending or using up energy.
Daily energy expenditure varies significantly across age groups.
Correlation ≠ causation
a mutual relationship or connection between two or more things.
A statistical correlation does not prove a direct causal link.
Prevalence ≠ rarity
the fact or condition of being widespread.
The prevalence of childhood health conditions prompted extensive research.
Caloric ≠ nutritional
relating to heat or energy value of food.
Caloric balance depends on both diet and physical activity.
Re-evaluation ≠ acceptance
the process of assessing something again.
Scientific progress relies on constant re-evaluation of old models.
Sedentary ≠ active
tending to spend much time seated; somewhat inactive.
A sedentary lifestyle contributes to energy imbalance.
Longitudinal ≠ snapshot
involving information about the same subjects over a long period of time.
Longitudinal studies provide robust evidence across decades.

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Upper-Intermediate (B2))

Use modal + have + past participle to speculate about past scientific assumptions (e.g., Earlier researchers might have overlooked key metabolic variables).

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
They might overlooked the data. → They might have overlooked the data. (Past speculation modal structure: 'might have' + past participle.)
The theory was prove wrong. → The theory was proven wrong. (Passive voice requires past participle 'proven' or 'proved'.)
