🎬 Session Vocabulary

1. Generic Discussion Vocabulary

royal duties
the official obligations, public appearances, and strict protocol required of a monarch or princess.
Princess Ann feels overwhelmed by her endless schedule of royal duties while touring European capital cities.
media ethics
the moral standards governing journalists regarding privacy, deception, and public interest.
Reporter Joe Bradley faces a test of media ethics when he realizes his runaway companion is a disguised princess.
incognito adventure
exploring a public place without revealing one's true identity or noble title.
Ann escapes her embassy and spends a magical afternoon on an incognito adventure across Rome with Joe.
fleeting romance
a brief, passionate romantic connection that cannot last due to real-world obligations.
Joe and Princess Ann share a tender, fleeting romance during her twenty-four hours of freedom.
dignified sacrifice
surrendering personal happiness or desires to honor a higher duty or public role.
Ann makes a dignified sacrifice by returning to her embassy and choosing her royal crown over personal love.

2. Authentic Film Quotes & Slang

Mouth of Truth
(famous landmark / scene) the ancient Roman stone relief statue (*Bocca della Verità*) featured in the film's comedy moment.
Joe pretends to have his hand bitten off inside the Mouth of Truth, causing Princess Ann to scream and laugh in delight.
exclusive scoop
(journalism slang) an important news story published before any competing newspaper or media outlet.
Joe's editor promises him five thousand dollars if he can secure an exclusive scoop on the missing princess.
smothered by protocol
(character quote motif) the suffocating feeling of having every hour of life scheduled by royal advisers.
Ann breaks down in tears in her bedroom, crying that she is being smothered by protocol and formal handshakes.
Vespa ride
(iconic cinematic moment) driving a motor scooter through chaotic Roman streets and historical plazas.
Ann impulsively takes the handlebars of a Vespa ride through Italian traffic, accidentally knocking over sidewalk fruit stands.
Rome, by all means, Rome
(famous final dialogue quote) Princess Ann's answer when asked which European city she cherished most.
During her press conference, Princess Ann breaks protocol to declare that she will cherish her memory of "Rome, by all means, Rome".

🔵 Part 1 : Dialogue, Themes & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Screenplay Discussion
Deconstruct the character conversations and screenplay dynamics. Integrate Modal Verbs of Obligation & Duty into your analysis.
Princess Ann feels suffocated by her demanding schedule of royal duties across European capitals. How does her midnight escape from the embassy break her golden cage?
★ Have you ever felt an overwhelming urge to escape your daily routine and responsibilities?
Reporter Joe Bradley faces a moral test of media ethics when he realizes he is spending the day with a disguised princess. Why does he keep his identity secret at first (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ Is it ever acceptable for a journalist or creator to use deception to get a great story?
Ann spends a glorious afternoon on an incognito adventure through Rome, eating gelato and getting a short haircut. How does shedding her royal persona transform her confidence?
★ How can changing your appearance or stepping into a new environment refresh your sense of self?
Joe and Ann share a sweet, fleeting romance during their twenty-four hours together. Why is their love story so poignant despite knowing it cannot last?
★ Why are brief, temporary connections in life often remembered more fondly than long relationships?
At the end of her holiday, Ann makes a dignified sacrifice by returning to her royal duties. How does her day of freedom change her posture when meeting diplomats?
★ How can a short period of personal growth strengthen your ability to handle difficult duties?
Joe tricks Ann at the ancient mouth of truth stone statue, pretending his hand was bitten off. How does this improvised comedy moment highlight their playful bond?
★ Do you enjoy lighthearted pranks and spontaneous humor when getting to know someone?
Joe's editor promises a huge cash bonus for an exclusive scoop accompanied by secret photos. Why does Joe ultimately choose not to publish the story?
★ What causes a person to value personal integrity and respect over financial gain or fame?
Ann complains to her doctors about feeling 'smothered by protocol' before sneaking out her window. How do rigid expectations drain a person's vitality?
★ How do you maintain your personal enthusiasm when facing rigid rules and repetitive routines?
Ann impulsively takes the wheel of a motor scooter, taking Joe on a wild vespa ride through Roman traffic. How does this famous scene capture the joy of spontaneous living?
★ What is the most spontaneous, adventurous thing you have ever done while traveling?
During her press conference, Princess Ann declares that she cherished 'Rome, by all means, Rome' above all other cities. How does this subtle code message signal her love for Joe?
★ How can a public statement carry a hidden, intimate meaning meant for only one person?
🟣 Cinema Spotlight : Scene Study & Adaptation▲

Compare the adaptation differences and visual contrasts in this film.

Original Screenplay & Blacklist History
• Written by Dalton Trumbo while blacklisted during the Hollywood Red Scare; fellow writer Ian McLellan Hunter fronted for him to receive the Oscar credit.
• The script subverted classic fairy-tale tropes by giving Princess Ann personal maturity and duty rather than a traditional marriage.
Visual Directing & Location Shooting
• Filmed entirely on location in Rome, using real landmarks like the Trevi Fountain, Pantheon, Spanish Steps, and Palazzo Colonna.
• Crisp black-and-white cinematography captures the architectural light and shadows of Italian plazas without colorful distractions.
🟢 Part 2 : Artistry, Interpretation & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Artistic Analysis
Analyze the cinematography, visual motifs, directorial choices, and narrative themes. Integrate Modal Verbs of Obligation & Duty into your responses.
Director William Wyler chose to film on location in Rome in black-and-white rather than color. How does black-and-white cinematography enhance the timeless, romantic feel of the city (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ Do you think black-and-white photography gives romantic films a more classic, poetic quality?
Audrey Hepburn won an Academy Award for her debut starring role as Princess Ann. How does her natural grace and expressive face capture both royal dignity and youthful wonder?
★ What qualities make a debut performance by an unknown actor captivate international audiences?
Gregory Peck requested that Audrey Hepburn receive equal star billing on the film posters, predicting she would win an Oscar. How does their onscreen chemistry reflect mutual respect?
★ How does genuine real-life respect between co-stars elevate their romantic chemistry on screen?
Eddie Albert provides hilarious comic relief as Irving Radovich, the secret photographer taking photos with a lighter-camera. How does his character balance the central romance?
★ What role do funny sidekick characters play in preventing romantic dramas from becoming overly sentimental?
The final press conference scene in the Palazzo Colonna is executed with minimal dialogue and powerful eye contact. How does visual acting convey their heartbreaking farewell?
★ How can unspoken glances between actors convey more emotion than a long dramatic speech?
The movie ends with Joe walking slowly and alone down the long, empty hall of the palace. Why is this quiet, somber walk a more powerful ending than a fairy-tale kiss?
★ Why do realistic, bittersweet movie endings often stay with us longer than traditional happy endings?
How does Roman Holiday subvert the traditional 'Cinderella' fairy tale by having the princess rescue herself through personal maturity?
★ Do you prefer fairy-tale stories where the protagonist achieves independence rather than just romantic rescue?
The film turned the Vespa motor scooter and the Spanish Steps into global cultural symbols. How does cinema shape our travel dreams and perception of real-world cities?
★ Has a movie ever inspired you to travel to a specific city or landmark? Tell us about it.
Compare Princess Ann's emotional breakdown in her embassy bedroom at the start with her calm authority when confronting her advisers at the end. What caused this transformation?
★ How can experiencing genuine freedom and love give someone the strength to stand up to authority?
If you were directing a modern version of Roman Holiday today, what contemporary public figure or royal persona would you base the main character on?
★ How would a high-profile public figure navigate sneaking away incognito in today's world of smartphones and social media?

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
If they would have warned us, we would know→If they had warned us, we would knowUse past perfect (had + past participle) in the if-clause of a mixed conditional.
He insisted she goes to Runway→He insisted she go to RunwayVerbs of demand/insistence like 'insist' trigger the subjunctive/bare infinitive.
I'm used to watch movies in original→I'm used to watching movies in originalThe phrase 'be used to' is followed by a gerund (-ing form), not a bare infinitive.
