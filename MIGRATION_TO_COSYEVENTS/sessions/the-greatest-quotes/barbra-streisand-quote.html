Barbra Streisand is the person who shattered conventional Hollywood standards with her extraordinary voice and fierce, uncompromising individuality, and in this session they focus on the following quote: "I knew that with a mouth like mine, I just hadda be a star or something." Why do we allow a conformist industry to define what is beautiful and worthy? If our most unconventional traits are actually our greatest strengths, how do we build the courage to stand out? Join us for an inspiring discussion on self-acceptance.

☕ The Philosophers' Ledger: Café Debates

"Let us see how many interpretations exist! There is never only one correct answer to any profound human question." : Oscar Wilde

📖 Session Vocabulary

Self-image
the way a person sees, values, and interprets their own appearance and character.
Refusing to get plastic surgery helped her maintain a highly healthy and proud self-image.
Unconventional
different from what is normal, expected, or standard in a social context.
Her voice and nose were unconventional by Hollywood standards, which made her a unique icon.
To stand out
to be noticeably different or much better than ordinary people or things.
Instead of trying to fit in, she chose to use her distinctive features to stand out in crowds.
Upbringing
the way a child is raised, educated, and socialized by their family.
Her difficult upbringing taught her to rely entirely on her own determination and talent.
Perception
the way people understand, see, or interpret a person or a cultural idea.
The general public perception of beauty was highly rigid when she first entered the entertainment industry.
Confidence
a strong belief in one's own abilities, appearance, or personal value.
Her performance on stage was full of confidence, silencing all of her critics instantly.
Stereotype
a fixed, oversimplified, and often unfair idea about how a certain group should look or act.
She constantly challenged the boring stereotype of how a female star ought to behave.
Identity
the unique combination of traits, values, and features that define who a person is.
Her nose was not a weakness, but a key component of her proud personal identity.
Ambition
a strong, continuous desire or goal to achieve something great through hard work.
Her quiet ambition was backed by an extraordinary work ethic and vocal talent.
Insecurity
uncertainty, anxiety, or lack of confidence about oneself or one's appearance.
Every star has some level of insecurity, but she refused to let it guide her choices.

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Intermediate (B1))

Master when to use a gerund (verb-ing) or an infinitive (to + verb) after verbs commonly associated with creative expression (creative, risk, enjoy, continue, stop, recommend).

🟣 Let's Speak Together : The Identity Spectrum 🌟▲

Discuss with your partner: Which path is most fulfilling for a creative person in the long term? Rank them:

🌟 Mainstream Stardom
Achieving massive global fame, meeting standard expectations, but sacrificing a part of your personal identity.
🎨 Niche Artistry
Remaining completely unconventional, enjoying a small loyal following, and staying 100% genuine.
🏢 The Backstage Genius
Using your visionary skills behind the scenes to guide others, avoiding the public spotlight and pressure.
🛠️ The Independent Builder
Creating your own small business or brand entirely on your own terms, accepting heavy initial responsibility.

Use gerunds and infinitives (enjoy standing out, refuse to conform) to justify your choices!

🏁 Closing Circle: Complete the Sentence▲

Finish the session by going in a circle. Each student must complete the following sentence with exactly one adjective or short phrase using the vocabulary learned:

"Standing out is..."

Examples: about confidence, unconventional, a key ambition, part of our identity, better than meeting stereotypes, overcoming insecurity.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
He have went to Paris → He has gone to Paris (Present perfect third-person singular 'has' + past participle 'gone'.)
We discussed about the problem → We discussed the problem / had a discussion about the problem ('Discuss' is transitive and takes no preposition, or use 'have a discussion about'.)
I am agree with you → I agree with you (Use 'agree' directly as a verb rather than using it with the auxiliary 'am'.)
