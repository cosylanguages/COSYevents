Food, Health, & Modern Habits

Compare a life reliant on quick fast food with a life centered on fresh home cooking. Explore convenience, nutrition, budget, and physical health.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Convenience ≠ hassle
the state of being useful, easy, or quick to do.
Fast food offers unparalleled convenience during busy workdays.
Nutrition ≠ junk
the process of providing or obtaining the food necessary for health.
Balancing convenience with proper nutrition is a challenge.
Processing ≠ fresh
the alteration of food items from their natural state.
Ultra-processed foods often contain excess sodium and preservatives.
Affordability ≠ expense
the ability to be bought without costing too much money.
The affordability of fast food attracts budget-conscious students.
Craving ≠ aversion
a powerful desire for something, especially food.
Late-night fast food cravings are driven by high sugar and fat content.
Calorie ≠ empty
a unit of energy in food.
Fast food meals can contain an entire day's recommended calorie intake.
Addictive ≠ wholesome
causing a strong, compulsive reliance.
Combination of salt and fats makes fast food highly addictive.
Moderation ≠ excess
the avoidance of excess or extremes.
Enjoying fast food in moderation poses fewer health risks.
Substitutes ≠ originals
a person or thing acting in place of another.
Healthy fast-casual bowls are popular substitutes for burgers.
Obesity ≠ wellness
the state of being grossly overweight.
High fast food consumption is linked to rising rates of obesity.

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Intermediate (B1))

Use contrast connectors to weigh pros and cons (e.g., Although fast food is quick, it lacks nutritional value).

📝 Task 1 : Part B: Structured Practice:

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Despite it is cheap, it is unhealthy. → Despite its low cost / Although it is cheap, it is unhealthy. ('Despite' requires a noun phrase or gerund, not a subject-verb clause.)
Fast food makes me to feel tired. → Fast food makes me feel tired. (The verb 'make' in causative sense takes a bare infinitive: 'make me feel'.)
