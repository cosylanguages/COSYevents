# Cinema Club Pedagogical & Content Style Guide

This style guide establishes strict, non-negotiable standards for all Cinema Club session content across all languages and regional variants (American English, British English, French, Spanish, Russian) in `sessions/cinema-club/`. It serves as the master reference for content creation, quality assurance, and automated auditing.

---

## Hard Rule 1: Real Dictionary Definitions & Film-Specific Example Sentences

**Hard Rule:** Every vocabulary entry must have a real, concise dictionary-style definition in the target language, paired with an example sentence that references a specific, verifiable moment, scene, or core theme from the film.

* **Target Language Definitions:** Definitions must explain the actual meaning of the word or phrase in clear, standard dictionary language (e.g., *"hierarchy: a system in which members of an organization or society are ranked according to relative status or authority"*).
* **Film-Specific Examples:** Example sentences must describe real character actions, plot points, or thematic elements (e.g., *"Andy Sachs quickly realizes that Miranda Priestly demands absolute submission from everyone on the Runway editorial team"*).
* **Prohibition of Boilerplate Fillers:** Using generic filler definitions (e.g., *"The core thematic concept/main theme of [WORD] as explored and highlighted in '[FILM]'"*) or generic example sentences (e.g., *"The narrative of '[FILM]' brilliantly dissects [WORD] to build character depth..."*) is strictly prohibited.

---

## Hard Rule 2: Authentic Slang & Film Quotes (No Fake Padding)

**Hard Rule:** The "Authentic Film Quotes & Slang" section must exclusively contain terms, idioms, catchphrases, or slang that are genuinely colloquial, idiomatic, or distinctive to that film's dialogue or setting.

* **Genuine Colloquialisms Only:** Words must be real slang or idiomatic expressions (e.g., *"clackers"* in *The Devil Wears Prada*, *"break a leg"* in *Funny Girl*, *"radin"* in *Radin*). Ordinary vocabulary (e.g., *silence*, *forbidden*, *scholar*, *beauty*, *banquier*) must **never** be placed in the Slang & Quotes section.
* **No Artificial Padding:** If a film's screenplay supplies only 2 or 3 genuine slang items or memorable quotes, include only those real items. Do **not** pad the section with ordinary vocabulary to reach an arbitrary count of 5.
* **Accurate Context Notes:** Definitions for slang entries must explain what the term means in conversational context and how or why it is used in the film (e.g., *"clackers: (slang) a derogatory nickname used by Runway staff for style-obsessed assistants whose high heels clack against the office floors"*). Boilerplate explanations (e.g., *"An authentic slang term... to establish context"*) are strictly banned.

---

## Hard Rule 3: 100% Unique, Film-Specific Discussion Questions (No Template Recycling)

**Hard Rule:** No sentence frame, question stem, or discussion prompt may be repeated verbatim (with only nouns or film titles swapped) across two different films in any language.

* **Verifiable Film References Required:** Every question in Part 1 and Part 2 must reference a concrete, verifiable aspect of the film — such as a specific character dynamic, plot turning point, directorial choice, visual motif, or script line.
* **Prohibition of Slotting Templates:** Slotting a movie title or vocabulary word into pre-existing generic question frames (e.g., *"How does the opening of '[FILM]' show the idea of [WORD]?"* or *"'[CHARACTER]'s story of [WORD] is very sad...' Do you agree?"*) is strictly banned.

---

## Rule 4: Natural Grammar Focus Integration (No Mechanical Sentence Tags)

**Hard Rule:** The targeted grammar structure must be woven naturally into questions where it fits syntactically and logically.

* **Seamless Syntactic Weaving:** The question itself should prompt or incorporate the grammar structure naturally (e.g., when focusing on *Imperatives & Softened Directives*: *"If you were advising Andy Sachs before her first morning at Runway, how would you phrase your warnings without sounding overly harsh?"*).
* **Banned Sentence Tags:** Mechanically appending fixed tags to the end of unrelated questions (e.g., *"Do you agree? Use Imperatives."*, *"Discuss using Gerunds."*, *"Respond with Conditionals."*) is strictly prohibited.

---

## Rule 5: Substantive Cinema Spotlight (Scene Study & Adaptation)

**Hard Rule:** The "Cinema Spotlight — Scene Study & Adaptation" section (`#s-lst`) must contain real, substantive educational content comparing specific adaptation changes or visual directorial choices.

* **Concrete Adaptation Points:** If the film is adapted from a book, play, or historical event, list specific plot, character, or tonal changes made by the filmmakers.
* **Directorial & Visual Analysis:** If the film is an original screenplay, detail specific visual choices — such as camera movement, color palette, sound design, or set construction — that shape the audience's experience.
* **Prohibition of Empty Header Containers:** Displaying empty grid boxes with only section titles (e.g., *"Original Screenplay Adaptations"* / *"Visual Directing & Setting Contrasts"*) without supporting bullet points or narrative text is strictly prohibited.

---

## Rule 6: Validated Specific Resource & Trailer Links

**Hard Rule:** Every trailer or clip link in the session snapshot must point directly to a specific, playable video URL (on YouTube, Vimeo, or official distributor channels).

* **Direct Video URLs Required:** Links must take learners directly to an official trailer or scene clip (e.g., `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6ZOZw3ofJHg`).
* **Banned Search Query Placeholders:** Generic search URLs (e.g., `https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Yentl+trailer`) or broken links are strictly banned.

---

## Rule 7: Genre Tone-Matching Matrix

**Hard Rule:** Content creators and session designers must adapt the register and tone of discussion questions to match the film's genre, emotional weight, and artistic intent.

| Genre Category | Representative Films | Question Register & Pedagogical Focus | Banned Registers & Misalignments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Comedic & Satirical** | *The Devil Wears Prada*, *Radin*, *She's the Man*, *Shes the Man* | Lighthearted, witty, examining social irony, ambition, character flaws, corporate absurdities, and comedic timing. | Sensationalized trauma framing, overly grim existential gloom (e.g., *"quiet burden of grief"*). |
| **Musicals & Period Romances** | *Yentl*, *Funny Girl*, *Mamma Mia*, *Serebryanye Konki* | Expressive, artistic, exploring self-expression, societal constraints, passion, theatrical flair, and cultural tradition. | Cold corporate jargon, clinical psychological diagnosis. |
| **Horror, Thriller & Sci-Fi** | *A Quiet Place*, *The Substance*, *Killing Eve*, *La Valla*, *The Others* | Tension-focused, analyzing suspense, survival dilemmas, psychological manipulation, environmental dread, and atmosphere. | Light comedic banter, triviating self-help clichés. |
| **Serious Drama & Historical** | *The Pianist*, *Still Alice*, *Incendies*, *Queendom*, *L'attente* | Empathetic, respectful, handling trauma, war, illness, loss, or systemic oppression with emotional maturity and care. | Sensationalized "dramatic" exaggeration, trivializing character suffering. |

---

## Rule 8: Banned Phrases & Template Frames

The following 12 exact template phrases and sentence frames are **strictly banned** from all Cinema Club session files:

1. **`"The core thematic concept/main theme of [WORD] as explored and highlighted in '[FILM]'"`**
2. **`"The narrative of '[FILM]' brilliantly dissects/scrutinizes [WORD] to build character depth as..."`**
3. **`"An authentic slang term, colloquialism, or key dialogue featured in '[FILM]' to establish context"`**
4. **`"A common expression used in '[FILM]' to establish context."`**
5. **`"The characters in '[FILM]' use the expression '[WORD]' during a pivotal scene inside [SETTING] to emphasize the drama."`**
6. **`"Deconstruct the visceral opening sequence of '[FILM]'. How does the visual establishment of [WORD] within [SETTING] initiate the dramatic tension?"`**
7. **`"When we first enter the world of [SETTING], how does the opening scene establish tension through [WORD]?"`**
8. **`"How does the opening of '[FILM]' show the idea of [WORD]?"`**
9. **`"If you were in '[FILM]', how would you handle the challenges of [WORD]?"`**
10. **`"'[CHARACTER]'s story of [WORD] in '[FILM]' is very sad, and they should be more careful.' Do you agree?"`**
11. **`"If you were a director, how would you show the concept of [WORD]?"`**
12. **Mechanical Grammar Tags:** `"Use [Grammar]."`, `"Practice with [Grammar]."`, `"Respond with [Grammar]."`, `"Frame your answer with [Grammar]."`
