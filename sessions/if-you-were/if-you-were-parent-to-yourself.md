round_1:
  title: 'Round 1 : Personal Depth'
  badge: Narrative
  instruction: ''
  items:
    - main: If you could travel back in time to meet your teenage self, what specific words of <strong>validation</strong> would you offer them first?
      personal: ★ What was your teenage self desperately waiting to hear from an adult?
    - main: How would you <strong>nurture</strong> your younger self during their most painful heartbreak or rejection?
      personal: ★ Did you have to carry that early pain alone, or did someone guide you?
    - main: What kind of self-imposed <strong>discipline</strong> would you teach your past self to prevent them from making your worst financial or personal mistakes?
      personal: ★ Were you too lazy or too hard on yourself during your early twenties?
    - main: How would you break the <strong>generational</strong> cycle of silent anxiety or strict expectations that you inherited from your real parents?
      personal: ★ In what way are you turning into your mother or father, even when you try not to?
    - main: If your younger self made a massive, embarrassing mistake, how would you demonstrate true <strong>unconditional</strong> love and <strong>forgiveness</strong>?
      personal: ★ Are you still holding a grudge against yourself for something you did years ago?
    - main: How would you teach your past self to handle emotional <strong>vulnerability</strong> without hiding behind a protective wall?
      personal: ★ Did your family view crying as a sign of weakness or a healthy release?
    - main: What boundaries would you set for your younger self regarding screen time, friendships, or work-life balance?
      personal: ★ Did your real parents let you stay up too late or play too many video games?
    - main: How would you help your past self process a childhood <strong>trauma</strong> that still echoes in your adult reactions today?
      personal: ★ What trigger instantly turns your adult mind back into a defensive child?
    - main: Do you believe you would have developed a stronger sense of <strong>resilience</strong> if you had received more gentle guidance instead of harsh criticism?
      personal: ★ Does criticism actually build character, or does it just build a mask?
    - main: If you were a <strong>compassionate</strong> parent to yourself right now, what permission would you grant your adult self today?
      personal: ★ Are you currently working too hard because you are still trying to prove your worth?

round_2:
  title: 'Round 2 : Speculative Analysis'
  badge: Projections
  instruction: ''
  items:
    - main: The ultimate act of self-care is learning to become a <strong>compassionate</strong> parent to your own inner child.
      personal: ★ Can we ever truly heal our childhood wounds without this internal work?
    - main: Most people spend their adult lives trying to fulfill the unachieved dreams of their real parents.
      personal: ★ Are your career goals actually yours, or are they a gift to your mother or father?
    - main: If you had parented yourself, you would have grown up with much more <strong>resilience</strong> but less creative hunger.
      personal: ★ Does a certain amount of childhood neglect or friction drive great artistic success?
    - main: We cannot offer true <strong>forgiveness</strong> to our real parents until we learn to parent ourselves first.
      personal: ★ Is blaming our parents just a lazy way to avoid taking adult responsibility?
    - main: Modern parenting has become too soft, failing to teach children the cold self-<strong>discipline</strong> required to survive in a competitive world.
      personal: ★ Do children need a dose of parental hardness to prepare for life's realities?
    - main: If you were your own parent, you would have forced your younger self to learn a musical instrument or a second language.
      personal: ★ Should parents ignore a child's protests and force them to practice difficult skills?
    - main: The concept of <strong>generational</strong> trauma is often used as an excuse for bad adult behavior.
      personal: ★ At what age must a person stop blaming their upbringing for their current actions?
    - main: Unconditional validation is dangerous because it prevents a child from recognizing their own toxic traits.
      personal: ★ Should a loving parent be their child's harshest critic?
    - main: If you had received more <strong>unconditional</strong> support as a teenager, you would be a completely different person today.
      personal: ★ Would you be happier, or would you lack the drive that got you here?
    - main: We are all parenting our past, present, and future selves every single time we make a hard decision.
      personal: ★ Is adulthood just a continuous conversation between our seven-year-old and eighty-year-old self?

mistakes:
  - wrong: If I <u>parented</u> myself back then, I would feel better now.
    right: If I <strong>had parented</strong> myself back then, I would feel better now.
    note: '(Grammar: Mixed conditional required for past hypothetical actions having present consequences: ''If + past perfect'' for past condition, ''would + bare infinitive'' for present result)'
  - wrong: We need to nurture <u>ourselves</u> inner child.
    right: We need to nurture <strong>our</strong> inner child.
    note: '(Grammar: Pronoun choice error: use the possessive determiner ''our'' before nouns)'
