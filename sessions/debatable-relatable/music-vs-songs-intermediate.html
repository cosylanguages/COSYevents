Instrumental Music vs Songs with Lyrics

10 March 2027

Home / Events / Debatable & Relatable / Music vs Songs (Intermediate)
← Back to Club

Duration

60 minutes

Languages

🇬🇧 English

Level

Intermediate (B1)

Topic

Music Preferences and Emotional Response

This session hosts a lively debate on how we experience and process musical sounds. We contrast purely instrumental compositions-which rely on instruments alone to convey meaning-with songs that incorporate explicit lyrics and human vocals. We will debate whether words restrict our creative imagination and explore how different soundscapes alter our mental focus.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Instrumental
music enacted without human vocals or lyrics.
Purely instrumental tracks are highly effective for maintaining deep study focus.
Lyrics
the words of a song, typically structured with rhymes.
Complex poetic lyrics can completely transform an otherwise simple pop melody.
Vocalist
a singer, especially in a jazz, classical, or popular music band.
The band hired a talented new vocalist to record their second studio album.
Melody
a sequence of single notes that is musically satisfying and memorable.
The haunting violin melody stayed in my mind for days after the concert.
Evocative
bringing strong images, memories, or feelings to mind.
Her acoustic guitar compositions are highly evocative of quiet, rainy afternoons.
Lyricist
a person who writes the lyrics or words for songs.
Bob Dylan is celebrated worldwide as one of the greatest lyricists in history.
Ambient
relating to the immediate surroundings; quiet background music.
They played soft ambient nature sounds in the spa treatment rooms.
Composition
a work of music, literature, or art.
The pianist played an original composition dedicated to his childhood teacher.
Resonance
the quality in a sound of being deep, full, and lasting.
The cathedral's high stone ceilings gave the choir a powerful acoustic resonance.
Interpretation
the action of explaining or finding the personal meaning of something.
Every listener brings their own subjective interpretation to abstract instrumental music.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • Do you focus better when studying with purely instrumental music or with lyric-heavy songs?
  • What makes a song's story feel deeply relatable to your own personal life?
🔵 Round 1 : Personal Exploration▲
Why do some people prefer instrumental compositions when they feel emotionally overwhelmed?
★ What instrument do you think has the most soothing, therapeutic sound?
Do explicit lyrics restrict our personal interpretation of a piece of music?
★ Can you share a song whose lyrics have had a major impact on your perspective?
Does a skilled vocalist have more power to move an audience than a brilliant pianist?
★ Who is your absolute favorite vocalist of all time, and why?
What neural processes make a beautiful melody stay stuck in our memories for days?
★ Have you ever had a song melody trigger a sudden, vivid childhood memory?
Why are certain natural or industrial sounds highly evocative of quiet loneliness?
★ What environmental sound (e.g. rain, train tracks) do you find most comforting?
How does the role of a professional lyricist differ from that of a traditional poet?
★ Do you ever write down your thoughts or poems when you are stressed?
Should modern workplaces play ambient music to reduce employee burnout?
★ Do you listen to ambient sounds or white noise when sleeping or working?
What makes a classical composition survive stably across hundreds of years?
★ Do you ever attend live classical concerts or opera performances?
How does the acoustic resonance of a physical hall alter our enjoyment of a show?
★ Do you prefer listening to music on high-quality speakers or small headphones?
Why is our interpretation of abstract jazz compositions so subjective and diverse?
★ Has a friend ever completely misunderstood your emotional mood or intentions?
🟣 Let's Speak Together : The Soundscape Design▲

The Soundscape Task: Work as a team to design the perfect sensory soundscape for a public library. Will you choose natural ambient sounds, acoustic instrumental compositions, or low-volume songs? Defend your creative choices!

🟢 Round 2 : Agree or Disagree: The Future▲
In the future, purely instrumental music will be fully customized in real time by our brainwaves. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you wear a device that changes your music style based on your active stress levels?
Artificial intelligence will soon write more poetic lyrics than any human lyricist. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you listen to a band if you knew all their songs were written by an AI?
A holographic vocalist will host the most popular live concerts of the next decade. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you buy a ticket to see a holographic performance of a historical band?
A complex, unstructured melody is superior to a simple, repetitive pop chorus. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Do you find simple pop songs to be boring or highly enjoyable?
Future cities will use highly evocative natural soundscapes to calm road rage and traffic stress. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Should cities play soft forest sounds at major pedestrian crossings?
A great lyricist has a more profound impact on society than a brilliant political leader. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Has a song ever changed your political or social beliefs?
Our homes will automatically play personalized ambient music based on our physical fatigue levels. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Would you like your home to automatically play calm music when you walk inside?
Classical composition techniques will become completely obsolete due to automated music generators. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Do you think computer-generated classical music lacks real human artistic soul?
The emotional resonance of live acoustic music can never be replicated by digital headphones. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Do you prefer attending small acoustic unplugged sessions or massive arena concerts?
A listener's subjective interpretation is more important than the original intention of the composer. : Do you agree or disagree?
★ Do you care about what a songwriter meant, or do you prefer your own interpretation?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
We discussed about the lyrics. → We discussed the lyrics / had a discussion about the lyrics. (Underlined error: 'discuss' is a transitive verb and takes a direct object without 'about')
I am agree with your interpretation. → I agree with your interpretation. (Underlined error: 'agree' is a verb, do not use it with auxiliary 'am')
The melody was very evocatively. → The melody was extremely evocative / very evocative. (Underlined error: use the adjective 'evocative' after the linking verb 'was' rather than the adverb 'evocatively')
