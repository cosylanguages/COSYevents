Part II: The Break-up : Week 1 to 4

Theme

Actionable Habit Reframing

In our final micro-discussion on Catherine Price's guidebook, we move from deconstruction to active transformation. We will evaluate the practical steps of the 30-Day Breakup Plan, analyzing how to triage our applications, establish firm digital boundaries, and manage compulsive cravings. This session focuses on rebuilding our offline reality and nourishing our analog, physical lives.

🗓️ 30-Day Breakup Action Plan Curriculum

Week 1: Technology Triage (Days 1 to 7)

Track screen time, pick up count, complete smartphone compaction self-assessment, delete social media apps, and establish physical boundaries (no phone in the bedroom).

Week 2: Changing Your Habits (Days 8 to 14)

Turn off all non-human notifications, establish firm tech-free zones, combat social phubbing (phone snubbing), cultivate mindful gaps, and replace pickup habits with an analog alarm clock.

Week 3: Reclaiming Your Brain (Days 15 to 21)

Practice the STOP technique (Stop, Take a breath, Observe, Proceed), retrain attention using analog books or solitary walks, prepare for and execute a 24-to-48-hour digital fast.

Week 4: Your New Relationship (Days 22 to 30)

Organize the home screen layout (categorized as Mindless, Tool, and Essential folders), establish strict curfews, batch email tasks, rebuild real-world social circles, and sustain analog habits.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Detox
the process of abstaining from or ridding the body of toxic substances or unhealthy habits, especially digital devices.
A digital detox is essential to recalibrate our chemical reward system and lower our anxiety levels.
Boundary
a limit or dividing line that defines where one space, activity, or relationship ends and another begins.
Establishing a strict bedroom boundary ensures that our sleep cycle remains completely protected from screens.
Triage
the process of sorting and prioritizing tasks, applications, or problems based on their urgency or value.
We must perform technology triage, deleting every application that steals our time without improving our life.
Habitual ≠ deliberate
done constantly or repeatedly as a matter of common practice and subconscious habit ≠ done with active intention and care.
Checking our screen is often habitual rather than deliberate, meaning we do it without any clear purpose in mind.
To reclaim
to retrieve, recover, or win back something valuable that was previously lost or taken away.
By putting away our devices during dinners, we can reclaim precious moments of genuine human connection.
Solitary
done or experienced completely alone, without the presence or participation of other people.
Engaging in a solitary walk allows our minds to relax and process daily events without virtual noise.
Offline ≠ connected
not actively utilizing or connected to a digital network or the global internet ≠ permanently online or receiving data.
We need more offline activities to balance our highly connected, screen-dominated work schedules.
To nourish
to provide with the substance, energy, or support necessary for growth, physical health, and general life.
We must find analog hobbies that nourish our creative spirit instead of consuming our time passively.
To implement
to put a decision, plan, or process into active effect or physical operation.
It is incredibly easy to read about detox plans, but highly challenging to implement them in our daily routine.
To succeed ≠ fail
to achieve the desired aim, result, or positive outcome of an endeavor ≠ to fall short of success or be unsuccessful.
If we set clear, realistic boundaries, we will succeed in building a healthy digital relationship, whereas ignoring rules causes us to fail.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Subjunctive and Modals for Recommendations▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (B2)

When making high-impact advice, use the subjunctive structure (it is crucial/essential/important that + subject + base verb) or strong modals like had better (followed by the bare infinitive). Note that the subjunctive verb has no third-person singular "s" or past endings.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
It is crucial that he implements the boundaries plan. → It is crucial that he implement the boundaries plan. (Underlined subjunctive verb. After 'it is crucial/essential/important that...', use the base verb form 'implement' without third-person 's')
We had better to delete the addictive applications. → We had better delete the addictive applications. (The modal structure 'had better' is followed directly by the bare infinitive verb, not the infinitive with 'to')
If we set limits, we will success in our detox. → If we set limits, we will succeed in our detox. (Underlined vocabulary choice: 'success' is a noun, whereas the verb form is 'succeed')
