Theme

Neurochemistry & Tech Design

We begin our serialized journey into Catherine Price's "How to Break Up with Your Phone" by deconstructing the physiological hooks of modern smartphones. This session focuses on the neurochemical manipulation engineered by application designers to capture our awareness. Together, we will analyze the relationship between intermittent rewards, dopamine anticipation, and compulsive checking habits.

📖 Active Book Curriculum Breakdown

  • Introduction: The Sirens of Your Screen - Deconstructing phantom vibrations and the automatic reach.
  • Chapter 1: The Slot Machine in Your Pocket - Variable intermittent rewards and phone design mechanics.
  • Chapter 2: Putting the Dope in Dopamine - Distinguishing immediate pleasure from motivational cravings.
📚 Literary Parlor : Dialogue Ledger Book Discussion Ledger

Featured Insight (Chapter 1 & 2): "Social media platforms are designed like slot machines, leveraging intermittent variable rewards to hijack our dopamine pathways."

Discussion Prompt: What is the first app you check when you pick up your phone, and what variable reward are you subconsciously expecting?

📖 Session Vocabulary

Addictive ≠ harmless
causing or likely to cause a strong habit or physical dependence ≠ safe or not causing any habit.
App developers design notifications to be addictive, turning a harmless tool into a constant compulsion.
Anticipation
the state of excitingly expecting or looking forward to something happening.
Our brains release dopamine in anticipation of a notification, not just when we read the message.
Dopamine
a chemical neurotransmitter in the brain associated with motivation, pleasure, and reward-seeking.
Checking social media triggers a tiny splash of dopamine, reinforcing our urge to refresh the feed.
Trigger ≠ suppress
to cause a particular reaction or process to begin suddenly ≠ to prevent a reaction or keep it under control.
The vibrating sound of a phone can trigger a sudden spike in our anxiety levels, while quiet environments suppress it.
Intermittent
stopping and starting at irregular intervals; not continuous or predictable.
Variable intermittent feedback is the primary mechanism used by slot machines to keep players engaged.
Reinforcement
the process of encouraging or establishing a belief or pattern of behavior.
Positive reinforcement occurs whenever we get a pleasant message after picking up our phone.
To crave
to feel a powerful, deep desire for something, often driven by habit or physical need.
When the screen goes dark, we begin to crave the next burst of colorful digital feedback.
To drip-feed
to supply or provide something to someone in very small, gradual quantities over time.
Social networks choose to drip-feed likes to your profile to maximize your total daily usage.
Mindless ≠ deliberate
done without active thought or consideration ≠ done consciously with a clear purpose and intent.
Mindless scrolling wastes hours of our day, whereas deliberate searches help us learn useful skills.
Compulsive
driven by an irresistible, repetitive urge to do something, regardless of consequences.
Many adults experience a compulsive need to check their phones immediately after waking up.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Present Perfect vs. Simple Past▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (B2)

Use the Present Perfect (have/has + past participle) to discuss life experiences, habits that started in the past and continue today, or actions with a direct connection to the present. Use the Simple Past for specific, finished actions in the past with a definite time reference.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I am addicted of scrolling social media. → I am addicted to scrolling social media. (Underlined prepositional error. Better collocation: the adjective 'addicted' always takes 'to')
This notification triggered a compulsive behavior yesterday. → This notification triggered compulsive behavior yesterday. (Grammar correction: 'behavior' is usually uncountable when referring to a general pattern of action)
We discussed about the dopamine loops. → We discussed the dopamine loops / had a discussion about the dopamine loops. (Prepositional error: 'discuss' is a transitive verb and does not take 'about')
