Theme

Neuroplasticity & Working Memory

In our third micro-discussion, we analyze the physiological damage of constant technological interruption. We will investigate the science of neuroplasticity, deconstructing how smartphones weaken our short-term working memory and degrade our capacity for deep focus. We will then explore mindfulness as a practical, reversible strategy to rebuild our attention span and reclaim natural spaces of solitary reflection.

📖 Active Book Curriculum Breakdown

  • Chapter 5: Your Phone Is Changing Your Brain - Neuroplasticity, working memory degradation, and task-switching.
  • Chapter 6: Your Phone Is Affecting Your Happiness & Health - Cortisol spikes, sleep cycles, and continuous partial attention.
  • Chapter 7: How to Take Back Your Life - Rediscovering boredom and solitude as psychological necessities.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Neuroplasticity
the physiological ability of the brain to reorganize itself by forming new neural pathways and structural connections throughout life.
Our brain relies on neuroplasticity to adapt to our daily habits, meaning constant distraction physically reshapes our mind.
To degrade ≠ improve
to lower the quality, strength, or physical condition of something over time ≠ to make it better or elevate its value.
Continuous digital alerts degrade our working memory, whereas active reading exercises improve our baseline concentration.
Mindfulness
the psychological state of being fully conscious, aware, and present in the current moment without judgment.
Practicing daily mindfulness enables us to recognize digital cravings without immediately satisfying them.
Focus
the center of activity, attraction, or attention, or the capacity to maintain concentration on a single task.
Our deep focus is shattered whenever we pause to check an incoming text message.
Memory
the faculty by which the mind stores, retains, and remembers past information and experiences.
Our working memory is severely limited, which is why we forget what we are doing after looking at our lock screen.
Reversible ≠ permanent
capable of being undone, altered, or returned to a previous state ≠ lasting or remaining unchanged forever.
Fortunately, tech-induced brain damage is reversible if we change our environment, whereas some neurological changes are permanent.
To sustain
to support, hold up, or maintain something physically or mentally for an extended period.
Without an alarm, it is highly challenging to sustain undivided attention on a difficult book.
Interruption
the sudden act of breaking the continuity or progress of a process, task, or conversation.
Every minor digital interruption costs us several minutes as our brain struggles to refocus.
Overwhelmed
feeling completely defeated, crushed, or physically and emotionally exhausted by an excessive amount of input or stress.
We often feel completely overwhelmed when we try to manage multiple work chats simultaneously.
Solitude
the state or situation of being entirely alone, often leading to a peaceful space for quiet reflection.
Smartphones have destroyed our capacity for quiet solitude, as we are constantly surrounded by virtual voices.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Modals of Possibility and Speculation▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (B2)

Use must to express strong certainty based on evidence. Use may or might to express realistic possibilities. Use could to discuss hypothetical possibilities in the present or future.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
This continuous alerts degrades my working memory. → These continuous alerts degrade my working memory. (Grammatical alignment: 'alerts' is plural, requiring 'these' and the plural verb form 'degrade')
We could sustained our focus yesterday. → We could sustain our focus yesterday. (Modal verb grammar: modal verbs like 'could' must be followed by the base infinitive verb, not the past participle)
I felt completely overwhelming by the messages. → I felt completely overwhelmed by the messages. (Underlined adjective choice. Use the '-ed' participle 'overwhelmed' to describe a passive feeling, and '-ing' to describe the active cause)
