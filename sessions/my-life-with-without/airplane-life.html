Topic

Aviation and Slow Travel

This session explores how aviation has completely transformed our sense of distance, geographical boundaries, and leisure. We will contrast the fast, modern lifestyle of frequent flying with the nostalgic, grounding philosophy of slow travel via trains and ships. Join us for a fascinating conversation about our lives with and without airplanes, and how fast transit impacts our mental well-being.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Aviation
the flying or operating of aircraft.
Modern aviation has made it possible to travel across continents in just a few hours.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • Do you enjoy the feeling of flying, or do you find airports stressful?
  • What is the furthest destination you have ever visited by plane?
🔵 Round 1 : Personal Depth▲
Narrative
How has modern aviation changed the way families stay connected across different continents?
★ Have you ever had to use aviation to visit a loved one who lives far away?
What is the most common complaint of a long-haul flight passenger regarding comfort and service?
★ When you are a passenger, how do you usually spend your time during a long flight?
Does reaching a distant destination very quickly make the travel experience feel less rewarding?
★ What is your dream travel destination that you hope to visit in the near future?
Why is physical jet lag such a strange, exhausting psychological experience for the brain?
★ How do you usually deal with or cure your jet lag after arriving in a new time zone?
How do you physically and mentally prepare for a exhausting, twelve-hour long-haul flight?
★ Have you ever taken a long-haul flight? What was the hardest part of the journey?
Is it genuinely possible for modern aviation to become eco-friendly, or is it destined to stay pollutive?
★ Do you feel guilty about the environmental cost of flying when you book a vacation?
Why is the philosophy of slow travel gaining so much popularity among stressed urban professionals?
★ Would you ever choose a slow, multi-day train journey over a quick two-hour flight?
Why are low-cost airlines so strict about their exact cabin baggage allowance guidelines?
★ Do you usually travel light, or do you always struggle with exceeding the baggage allowance?
How does being a frequent flyer alter your perspective on geography, borders, and cultural differences?
★ Do you consider yourself a frequent flyer, or do you travel only once or twice a year?
What is the best way to spend a long, boring layover in an unfamiliar international airport terminal?
★ Have you ever had a terrible or funny experience during an airport layover?
🟣 Let's Speak Together : The Slow Travel Project▲

The Train Voyage Task: Imagine airplanes have been banned worldwide for environmental reasons. Work as a group to design a slow travel route from London to Beijing using only trains, ships, and cars. What countries would you cross? Present your slow-paced adventure itinerary!

🟢 Round 2 : Speculative Analysis (Life WITHOUT Planes)▲
Projections
Imagine a world with absolutely no aviation-how would our global economy and remote supply chains survive?
Without planes, every international passenger would have to spend weeks traveling on ocean liners and trans-continental trains.
Reaching a distant destination would become a rare, once-in-a-lifetime achievement rather than a quick weekend plan.
The complete absence of planes would mean the end of physical jet lag, aligning our bodies with local geographic time.
Instead of booking quick weekend splurges, long-haul journeys would require deep, careful planning and months of free time.
A shift to trains and ships would make global tourism highly eco-friendly, drastically reducing atmospheric pollution.
The philosophy of slow travel would become a normal reality for everyone rather than a trendy lifestyle choice.
Without strict airport security and baggage allowance fees, packing would become much more relaxed and comfortable.
The prestigious concept of the frequent flyer would be replaced by slow, seafaring passengers sharing months-long voyages.
Instead of a quick airport layover, travelers would spend days resting in beautiful, historic train station hotels.
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
We traveled by the plane last summer. → We traveled by plane last summer. (Underlined error: when specifying a means of transport with 'by', do not use an article like 'the' or 'a')
The jet lag made me to feel very tired. → The jet lag made me feel very tired. (Underlined error: the causative verb 'make' takes a bare infinitive without 'to')
