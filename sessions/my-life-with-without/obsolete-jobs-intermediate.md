life_ledger:
  title: '⚖️ Dual-Perspective Balance Sheet: Obsolete Jobs (B1)'
  with_title: Presence (With)
  with_content: • Wide employment of manual workers across highly repetitive physical trades.<br>• Comforting, highly personalized human touch in daily public service.<br>• Dangerous, exhausting labor under harsh and unregulated working environments.
  without_title: Absence (Without)
  without_content: • Superior system efficiency, speed, and massive cost savings via automation.<br>• Safe working conditions and elimination of dangerous manual routines.<br>• Severe risk of sudden worker redundancy and cold, automated public service.
vocabulary:
  - word: Obsolete ≠ Current
    definition: no longer produced or used; out of date ≠ belonging to the present time; happening or being used now.
    example: While physical switchboards are completely obsolete today, smartphones remain current and ubiquitous.

  title: 'Round 1 : My Life With Obsolete Jobs'
  badge: Narrative &amp; Necessity
  instruction: ''
  items:
    - main: While the knocker-up is now completely <strong>obsolete</strong>, digital alarms are <strong>ubiquitous</strong>; how did people <strong>used to</strong> organize their morning cycles when relying on a human?
      personal: ★ Would you trust a person tapping on your window to wake you up today?
    - main: If we completely <strong>automate</strong> every service sector job, do we lose the valuable social connections we <strong>used to</strong> build in local neighborhoods?
      personal: ★ Do you prefer self-checkout machines or talking to a friendly supermarket cashier?
    - main: Does learning about extinct trades fill you with historical <strong>nostalgia</strong>, or do you prefer the convenience of progress?
      personal: ★ What is an old object or machine from your childhood that you miss?
    - main: Why did modern refrigeration completely <strong>displace</strong> ice cutters? How did families <strong>used to</strong> store food during hot summers?
      personal: ★ Have you ever had to survive without a working fridge for a few days?
    - main: Do neighborhood vendors who bring a comforting <strong>human touch</strong> feel necessary for a community's sanity?
      personal: ★ Do you know the name of any delivery person who comes to your house?
    - main: Is high industrial <strong>efficiency</strong> an absolute necessity, even if it directly leads to sudden worker <strong>redundancy</strong>?
      personal: ★ Have you ever experienced a system upgrade at work that made your job easier?
    - main: When the <strong>pervasive</strong> noise of machinery filled old factories, how did workers <strong>used to</strong> protect their sanity without modern ear defense?
      personal: ★ Do you work better in a silent room or with background noise?
    - main: Why did printing completely <strong>eradicate</strong> the town crier? How <strong>used to</strong> citizens get local news in real-time?
      personal: ★ How do you usually get your local news and community updates?
    - main: Phone operators served as the <strong>predecessor</strong> to networks; how did callers <strong>used to</strong> handle urgent emergencies when lines were manually connected?
      personal: ★ Do you prefer calling people on the phone or sending text messages?
    - main: Does an automated smart light lack the romantic <strong>aesthetic</strong> that old gas lamps <strong>used to</strong> bring to dark streets?
      personal: ★ Do you use candles in your house to create a relaxing atmosphere?

mistakes:
  - wrong: I am <u>used to wake up</u> early in the past.
    right: I <strong>used to wake up</strong> early.
    note: (Confusing habit in the past with current familiarity. Use 'used to + infinitive' for past habits.)
  - wrong: This technology is very <u>expired</u>.
    right: This technology is completely <strong>obsolete / outdated</strong>.
    note: '(Word choice: food or medicine expires, whereas technology becomes obsolete or outdated.)'
