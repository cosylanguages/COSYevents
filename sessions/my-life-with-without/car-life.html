The car has long been a symbol of personal freedom and autonomy, yet its dominance in urban planning is increasingly being questioned. This session explores the practicalities of car ownership versus the benefits of an active, car-free lifestyle. We will analyze how our mobility choices shape our daily stress levels, financial health, and the environment of our cities.

⚖️ Dual-Perspective Balance Sheet: Car Life

Presence (With)

• Unrestricted point-to-point mobility and spontaneous travel autonomy.
• Comfort of a private cabin shelter from cold/inclement weather.
• Regular financial outlays for fuel, maintenance, insurance, and parking.

Absence (Without)

• Drastic reduction in household overheads and zero depreciation stress.
• Healthier lifestyle driven by active transport (cycling, walking).
• Vulnerability to transit schedules, delays, and last-mile connectivity gaps.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Mobility
the ability to move or be moved freely and easily.
Owning a car provides a great sense of personal mobility.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • Do you have a driving license? If so, when did you get it?
  • What is the longest car journey you have ever taken?
🔵 Round 1 : My Life With a Car▲
Narrative & Necessity
How does owning a car provide a level of personal mobility that you simply cannot find on public transit?
★ Think about how you used to travel when you were a teenager-was it more or less comfortable?
Is a city's road infrastructure an absolute necessity, or does it exist because societies used to prioritize cars over pedestrian life?
★ Have you ever had a car breakdown at a bad time?
Why do car owners refuse to use public transport even if the network is faster than driving?
★ Are you satisfied with the transport in your city?
Can someone maintain good mental health if they have to sit in traffic congestion on their daily commute?
★ If your personality was a car, what model would it be?
Does the financial cost of regular car maintenance feel like a reasonable price for comfort, or does it feel like a burden?
★ Do you track your car expenses carefully?
Does owning a private car give you genuine autonomy, or are you actually dependent on its constant expenses?
★ Think about how your family used to plan weekend trips when you were young-did a car make it easier?
Is selling your car the only realistic way for a citizen to drastically lower their personal carbon footprint?
★ Do you prefer to walk or drive when running local errands?
Why do people subject themselves to a stressful daily commute in a private car instead of walking or cycling?
★ Was learning to drive a stressful experience for you?
How does high walkability in a neighborhood reduce the psychological need to own a private vehicle?
★ Do you care about the brand or look of the car you drive?
Why do millions of buyers accept the rapid financial depreciation of a new vehicle just to own a private cabin?
★ Would you feel comfortable sharing a ride with complete strangers?

🟢 Round 2 : My Life Without a Car▲
Speculative Adaptation
If personal cars were completely outlawed in city centers, how would communities redesign local urban mobility and transit systems?
★ What would be your biggest daily challenge without a car?
Without any cars to plan for, how could urban planners adapt city infrastructure to support vibrant pedestrian life?
★ Do you think you would know your neighbors better if you walked everywhere?
If private vehicles did not exist, how would local authorities adapt public transport to handle the massive passenger load?
★ Would you trust a car that has no steering wheel?
In a car-free city, would road congestion completely disappear, or would it simply shift to bike lanes and sidewalks?
★ What would you do with the extra space in your home?
If families didn't have to pay for car maintenance, what alternative local services or experiences would they spend their savings on?
★ Have you ever experienced "road rage"?
Without a private vehicle, would you lose your sense of personal autonomy, or would you find new ways to stay independent?
★ What is the one thing public transport can never replace for you?
In a fully car-free society, how would the commercial logistics network adapt to lower its collective carbon footprint?
★ What is the best place you have ever reached only by car?
Without roads dominating our landscape, would the daily commute to work become a highly active and social journey?
★ Was childhood neighborhood safe for playing in the street?
If streets were turned into parks, how would high walkability change the way neighbors communicate and support each other?
★ How would a massive increase in fuel prices change your lifestyle?
Since citizens wouldn't suffer from the financial depreciation of vehicles, how would they invest their capital instead?
★ Which city do you think is the best model for a car-free future?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I am drive a car since five years → I have been driving for five years (Present Perfect Continuous)
I prefer to drive than take the train → I prefer driving to taking the train (Prefer A to B)
It depends of the maintenance cost → It depends on the maintenance cost (Depends on)
I am not used to drive in city → I am not used to driving in the city (Be used to + -ing)
