This session explores the history and human impact of extinct careers-from the knocker-ups who woke up sleeping workers to the lectors who read literature in noisy cigar factories. vocabulary:
  - word: Anachronistic
    definition: belonging or appropriate to a period other than that in which it exists, especially old-fashioned.
    example: In our digital age, manual switchboards seem completely anachronistic.

round_1:
  title: 'Round 1 : My Life With Obsolete Jobs'
  badge: Theoretical Discussion &amp; Necessity
  instruction: ''
  items:
    - main: Not only did the knocker-up wake workers on time, but they also offered a highly personalized morning cycle; are modern ubiquitous alarms fundamentally <strong>anachronistic</strong> in their coldness?
      personal: ★ Have you ever felt a deep sense of vulnerability when realizing how dependent you are on digital alarms?
    - main: Not only did the <strong>mechanization</strong> of agriculture displace thousands of manual laborers, but it also permanently altered the demographic structure of metropolises; was this transformation necessary?
      personal: ★ What is the most physically demanding, repetitive labor you have ever personally performed?
    - main: Not only does uncritical <strong>sentimentality</strong> for old crafts blind us to their harsh conditions, but it also creates a false narrative of the past; how can we appreciate defunct jobs objectively?
      personal: ★ Is there an outdated craft or trade that you find deeply fascinating or artistically inspiring?
    - main: Not only do automated systems <strong>marginalize</strong> mature manual workers, but they also accelerate social alienation; should businesses preserve manual tasks as a matter of social health?
      personal: ★ Have you ever had to watch someone you care about struggle to adapt to new workplace systems?
    - main: Not only did physical letters offer a rich <strong>tactile connection</strong>, but they also forced us to slow down our social communication; is instant messaging making dialogue too shallow?
      personal: ★ Do you still find value in sending handwritten cards or letters on special occasions?
    - main: Not only is systematic corporate <strong>streamlining</strong> rendering human administrative cashiers a sad <strong>superfluity</strong>, but it is also making public spaces completely cold; can we reverse this?
      personal: ★ Have you ever worked in an environment where speed and metrics were prioritized over quality of interactions?
    - main: Not only did old manual jobs protect absolute personal privacy, but they also prevented the rise of <strong>omnipresent</strong> screens; is a digit-free career still viable?
      personal: ★ How do you find a sense of true solitude and privacy in an interconnected world?
    - main: Not only did modern news feeds completely <strong>eradicate</strong> the theatrical town crier, but they also replaced human projection with algorithms; is modern public communication superior?
      personal: ★ Do you feel overwhelmed by the constant stream of sensationalist news notifications?
    - main: Not only did manual switchboard operators act as the historic <strong>predecessor</strong> to automated networks, but they also kept human communication warm; was their redundancy inevitable?
      personal: ★ Have you ever had an intimate conversation with a stranger that changed your perspective?
    - main: Not only do coal-fires and oil-lamps have an unmatched <strong>aesthetic charm</strong>, but they also offer a highly comforting light; does the smart home feel sterile?
      personal: ★ What is the most visually atmospheric room or place you have ever stayed in?
