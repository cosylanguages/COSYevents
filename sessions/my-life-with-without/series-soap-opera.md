vocabulary:
  - word: Soap opera
    definition: a television drama series dealing with daily events in the lives of the same characters.
    example: My grandmother watches her favorite Spanish soap opera every single afternoon.

round_2:
  title: 'Round 2 : Speculative Analysis (Life WITHOUT TV Series)'
  badge: Projections
  instruction: ''
  items:
    - main: Imagine a world with absolutely no <strong>soap opera</strong> or dramatic television-how would retired seniors spend their quiet afternoons?
    - main: Without any multi-season <strong>series</strong>, people would have to wait for weekly theater plays or read long physical books for story satisfaction.
    - main: The complete absence of streaming apps would end <strong>binge-watching</strong>, forcing families to socialize more during winter weekends.
    - main: Without the constant stress of a weekly <strong>cliffhanger</strong>, our sleep patterns and mental health would improve significantly.
    - main: A world without internet forums would mean the end of the term <strong>spoiler</strong>, allowing friends to discuss shows freely at any time.
    - main: If screen stories lacked any sudden <strong>plot twist</strong>, would the art of writing novels become boring and highly predictable?
    - main: Without witnessing a long <strong>character arc</strong> on screen, we might find it harder to empathize with people who change slowly in real life.
    - main: A complete shift to realistic, non-<strong>melodramatic</strong> news would make public conversations much calmer and more logical.
    - main: Instead of watching a quick 40-minute <strong>episode</strong>, families would spend hours playing music or cooking meals together.
    - main: The absence of television would prevent us from developing an unhealthy emotional <strong>attachment</strong> to fictional characters.
mistakes:
  - wrong: I watched all <u>the series</u> yesterday.
    right: I watched the entire <strong>series</strong> / all <strong>the episodes</strong> yesterday.
    note: '(Underlined error: ''series'' is singular or plural, but when referring to individual parts of a show we say ''episodes'')'
  - wrong: This episode made me <u>to cry</u>.
    right: This episode made me <strong>cry</strong>.
    note: '(Underlined error: the causative verb ''make'' is followed by a bare infinitive without ''to'')'
