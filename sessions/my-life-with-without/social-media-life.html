Level

Intermediate (B1)

Social media has fundamentally rewired our social interactions, offering unprecedented connectivity while simultaneously introducing new psychological pressures. This session explores the balance between the digital world and real-life experiences, focusing on the impact of constant scrolling and online comparison on our mental health. We will imagine what our lives might look like if we returned to a pre-digital social landscape and how we can maintain an authentic identity in an algorithmic age.

⚖️ Dual-Perspective Balance Sheet: Social Media

Presence (With)

• Immediate global connectivity, real-time news, and community building.
• Instant platform for self-expression, branding, and professional promotion.
• Algorithmic distraction, compulsive scrolling, and unhealthy social comparison.

Absence (Without)

• Superior mental focus, higher attention span, and deeper quiet periods.
• Face-to-face, fully present conversations and authentic social connections.
• Risk of missing out on immediate viral trends or professional updates.

🗣️ Syntactic Anchor (CEFR B1): Practice expressing cause and effect relationships (e.g., "Constant notification triggers result in higher anxiety; conversely, deleting the app leads to deep mental peace") to discuss digital hygiene.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Doomscrolling
spending excessive time scrolling through bad news online.
I spent the whole evening doomscrolling instead of sleeping.
Detox
a period of time in which one abstains from unhealthy behaviors.
A weekend digital detox helped me feel more present.
Validation
recognition or affirmation that a person is worthwhile.
Many people seek validation through likes on social media.
FOMO
Fear Of Missing Out.
Social media can often trigger a sense of FOMO.
Engagement
the degree to which people interact with content.
High engagement rates are important for influencers.
Influencer
a person with the ability to influence potential buyers.
She became a successful travel influencer on Instagram.
Anonymous
not identified by name; of unknown name.
Many people post anonymous comments online.
Echo Chamber
an environment where a person only encounters information that reinforces their own.
Social media can easily become an echo chamber.
Comparison
estimate of the similarities or dissimilarities between two things.
Constant comparison with others can lead to unhappiness.
Authentic
of undisputed origin; genuine.
It is difficult to be truly authentic on social media.

🔵 Round 1 : My Life With Social Media▲
Narrative & Necessity
Does compulsive late-night use of mobile phones naturally lead to hours of unhealthy doomscrolling?
★ What was the hardest app to give up?
Does taking a voluntary digital detox always result in a significant boost in mental clarity and focus?
★ Do you think online connection is "good enough"?
Do modern careers and personal branding heavily depend on social media likes for psychological validation?
★ Do you check the news more often than you should?
Does constant exposure to edited online lives inevitably lead to a deep, persistent sense of FOMO?
★ Have you ever met a real friend online?
Do toxic social habits often stem from algorithms optimized purely for high user engagement?
★ What is the first app you open when you are bored?
Does the entire financial livelihood of a digital influencer directly result in biased or insincere recommendations?
★ What is one thing you would never post online?
Does posting on an anonymous forum frequently lead to uninhibited, hostile behavior among users?
★ Does it feel like a waste of time or a form of rest?
Does extreme political polarization directly stem from the algorithmic echo chamber on popular networks?
★ Which hobby have you neglected because of your phone?
Does the constant, unhealthy comparison of our daily lives with curated profiles always result in low self-esteem?
★ Are you more confident or more careful when posting?
Can an online user project a truly authentic persona, or does the medium naturally lead to a superficial mask?
★ Do you keep your phone out of the bedroom?

🟢 Round 2 : My Life Without Social Media▲
Speculative Adaptation
Without the temptation of feeds, what alternative late-night habits would people develop to avoid late-night doomscrolling?
★ Who is the first person you would call or visit if you couldn't message them online?
If social networks disappeared, would the need for a designated digital detox become completely obsolete?
★ Have you ever felt bad about your own life after looking at someone's perfect social media profile?
If online platforms were gone, what alternative channels would creators build to receive public validation for their art?
★ What is one hobby or activity you enjoy doing completely in private, without telling anyone?
Without real-time updates from acquaintances, how would people satisfy their curiosity and conquer their FOMO?
★ How did you find your favorite band, book, or movie-was it recommended by a friend or an algorithm?
If software success was measured by mental peace instead of user engagement, how would the web adapt?
★ Do you often see opinions online that make you genuinely angry or frustrated?
If the concept of a paid influencer was banned, what new local models of community trendsetting would emerge?
★ Have you ever written a comment or post anonymously, and did it change how you behaved?
If we completely stopped broadcasting our locations, how would our definition of an anonymous lifestyle transform?
★ What is one part of your life that you will absolutely never share on the internet?
Without any algorithmic echo chamber to divide us, would physical community debates become more constructive?
★ Do you follow any digital influencers or online content creators for inspiration?
Without constant, automated opportunities for social comparison, would people feel more content with their achievements?
★ Would you be willing to delete all your online accounts forever to protect your privacy?
Can humanity establish a more authentic way of connecting if we abandon algorithmic social networks forever?
★ Do you remember what you used to do during your free time before you owned a smartphone?
🏁 Closing "Mic Drop" Rituals▲

1. The Adjective Sentence Completion

Complete the sentence in exactly one turn:
"My life without social media would be ___________ because..."
(Examples: peaceful, boring, isolating, authentic, quiet, difficult, simpler)

2. The Year 2125 Technology Pivot

Imagine the year is 2125. Scientists invent a secure "Mind-to-Mind thought-transmission network" that allows you to instantly share memories and feelings with anyone globally. There are no screens, ads, or profiles.
Would you connect your brain to this thought-transmission network, or would you reject it and stay fully analog? Why?

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I spend many time on phone→I spend a lot of time on my phone(Uncountable time)
He is an influencer since long time→He has been an influencer for a long time(Present Perfect + for)
The social media is dangerous→Social media is dangerous(Generalization)
I am interesting in his posts→I am interested in his posts(-ed vs -ing)
I am agree with you→I agree with you(Verb agreement)
