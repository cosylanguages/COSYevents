Focus Adverbs & Gender-neutral Expressions

📜 Read Column Transcript▼

As a linguist, I spend my days dissecting grammar, vowels, and syntax. Recently, over a glass of dry red wine in Saint-Malo, I listened to a grand debate about inclusive French grammar, the endless battle of 'tous et toutes' and median periods. While sipping my own version of the drink from Gods, I couldn't help but wonder... We spend so much energy polishing our syllables, hoping to cure centuries of disparity with a suffix. But does rearranging our grammar genuinely foster equality, or is it merely an elegant distraction from the stubborn, cold realities of inequality?

📖 Session Vocabulary

Gender-neutral
suitable for, applicable to, or common to both male and female sexes.
Using gender-neutral pronouns like 'they' has become standard in modern business writing.
Inclusivity ≠ exclusion
the practice of providing equal access and opportunities to everyone ≠ shutting someone out.
The company's focus on inclusivity successfully eliminated the long-standing culture of exclusion.
Linguistic
relating to language or linguistics.
The French Academy is famous for its strict resistance to rapid linguistic changes.
Addressing
the act of speaking or writing to a person or group.
Addressing a crowd with 'tous et toutes' requires a conscious mental adjustment.
Equality
the state of being equal, especially in status, rights, and opportunities.
Linguistic reform is often seen as a minor step on the long road to true social equality.
Honorific
a title or word implying or expressing respect.
The teacher explained that choosing the correct honorific is essential in formal diplomacy.
Reform
make changes in something, typically a social, political, or economic institution.
Linguistic reform can trigger fierce debates among conservative grammarians.
Tokenism
the practice of making only a perfunctory or symbolic effort to do something.
Many critics argue that using inclusive greetings without policy changes is mere tokenism.
Disparity
a great difference, especially one of unfair or unequal treatment.
Despite inclusive language, the gender wage disparity in executive roles remains high.
Syllable
a unit of pronunciation having one vowel sound, with or without surrounding consonants.
Sometimes a single syllable in a pronoun can completely alter the vibe of a sentence.
