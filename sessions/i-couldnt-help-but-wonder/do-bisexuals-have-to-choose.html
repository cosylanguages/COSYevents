📰 Perspective Mirror

Does entering a long-term monogamous relationship mean choosing a side? This session examines societal assumptions around bisexuality, biphobia, fluid attraction, and personal identity. Special thanks to Cassandra Ardnassac for suggesting this thoughtful topic!

📖 Session Vocabulary

Erasure ≠ Visibility
the removal of all traces of something; social invisibility.
Bisexual erasure occurs when media ignores fluid attraction.
Binary ≠ Spectrum
something made of two distinct parts or choices.
Moving beyond the rigid binary allows for authentic self-expression.
Monogamy ≠ Non-monogamy
the practice or state of being in a relationship with one person at a time.
Choosing monogamy does not alter a person's underlying orientation.
Fluidity ≠ Fixedness
the quality of being likely to change; adaptability.
Sexual fluidity challenges rigid traditional categorizations.
Validation ≠ Rejection
recognition or affirmation that a person or feeling is valid.
Seeking social validation is natural but self-acceptance comes from within.
Identity ≠ Anonymity
the fact of being who or what a person is.
Sexual orientation is a core aspect of personal identity.
Dichotomy ≠ Continuum
a division or contrast between two things represented as opposed.
The false dichotomy of straight vs gay ignores bisexual reality.
Assumption ≠ Verified fact
a thing that is accepted as true without proof.
Societal assumptions often pressure people to pick a label.
Visibility ≠ Invisibility
the state of being visible or noticed by the public.
Increasing bisexual visibility helps break harmful stereotypes.
Stereotype ≠ Individual nuance
a widely held but fixed image or idea of a particular type of person.
Challenging outdated stereotypes builds genuine empathy.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up (10 mins)▲

Welcome! Share your initial thoughts on this topic. What personal experiences or observations come to mind right away?

⚡ Grammar Practice : Concessive Clauses & Complex Contrast (even if, regardless of, despite) (B2/C1)▲
Use concessive clauses to express nuanced perspectives: "Regardless of who they marry, a person's orientation remains valid." / "Even if society demands simple labels, identity is complex and fluid."
🔵 Round 1: Core Exploration (Units 1–10)▲
Why does society frequently force a false dichotomy between straight and gay identities, ignoring fluidity?
★ Have you ever felt pressured by others to categorize your preferences?
Does entering a long-term monogamy commitment mean a bisexual person has "chosen a side"?
★ Does your relationship status define who you are as a person?
How does cultural erasure affect the mental health and self-acceptance of bisexual individuals?
★ How can communities create more inclusive spaces for everyone?
Why do harmful stereotypes about indecisiveness or infidelity persist regarding bisexuality?
★ How do you handle unexamined assumptions when meeting new people?
How can genuine personal validation be maintained when external social recognition is lacking?
★ Where do you find your strongest sense of self-acceptance?
How does the assumption that attraction must be static complicate coming out at different life stages?
★ Have you ever changed your mind about a core belief or preference as an adult?
Why is bisexual visibility often erased in historical and biographical narratives?
★ How do historical stories shape your understanding of personal freedom?
In what ways do modern social media algorithms reinforce a rigid binary in dating and identity?
★ How do social media trends influence your daily perceptions of self-expression?
How can communities overcome intra-group erasure and foster genuine solidarity?
★ What makes you feel truly welcomed and valued in a social circle?
Why does the false dichotomy of "either/or" persist even in progressive discussions of human behavior?
★ How do you navigate situations where people demand simple answers to complex questions?
🟢 Round 2: Deep Speculation & Future Horizon (Units 11–20)▲
Why is breaking free from the rigid binary essential for understanding modern romantic identity?
★ How have your views on identity evolved over the past few years?
How can increasing bisexual visibility in media and education counteract societal biphobia?
★ What movie or book character challenged your understanding of identity?
Why do people often confuse an attraction orientation with a lifestyle choice?
★ What is the difference between who you are and what choices you make daily?
How can partners in a relationship support each other's authentic identity without insecurity?
★ What qualities build deep trust between romantic partners?
What advice would you offer to someone struggling with societal pressure to label their feelings?
★ What empowering message would you share with your younger self?
How will future societal shifts in gender norms influence the cultural fluidity of romantic attraction?
★ What social shift in your lifetime has surprised you the most?
Does institutional monogamy systematically favor binary orientations over fluid identities?
★ What traditional marriage or partnership customs do you find most meaningful?
How can educational curricula address bisexual stereotypes without reducing individuals to statistics?
★ What lesson from school had the most lasting impact on how you treat others?
How does external validation compare to self-anchored emotional resilience in facing social prejudice?
★ When facing skepticism from others, how do you protect your inner peace?
What will romantic and personal identity look like a generation from now as rigid labels decline?
★ What is one value or boundary you hope future generations preserve?
