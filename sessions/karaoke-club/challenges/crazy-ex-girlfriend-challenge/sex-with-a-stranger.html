📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Stranger
A person whom one does not know or with whom one is not familiar.
Hooking up with a stranger sounds romantic until your true crime brain kicks in.
Opposite: Acquaintance
Dangerous
Able or likely to cause harm or injury.
She convinced herself that a mild-mannered guy named Scott was a dangerous serial killer.
Opposite: Safe
Paranoia
Unjustified suspicion and mistrust of other people.
Watching too many murder documentaries induces acute, hilarious paranoia.
Opposite: Trust
Hotel
An establishment providing accommodation, meals, and other services.
A neutral hotel room feels like a crime scene waiting to happen when you are anxious.
Opposite: Home
Murderer
A person who commits murder.
Every strange noise made her suspect he was secretly an escaped murderer.
Opposite: Victim

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Excitement
A feeling of great enthusiasm and eagerness.
The initial excitement of casual dating was completely drowned out by fear.
Opposite: Boredom
Safe
Protected from or not exposed to danger or risk.
She texted her best friend her exact GPS location just to feel safe.
Opposite: Vulnerable
Intimacy
Close familiarity or friendship; closeness.
Attempting physical intimacy with someone whose last name you don't know is awkward.
Opposite: Distance
Kidney
Either of a pair of organs that filter blood, often featured in urban legends.
She worried she would wake up in an ice bath missing an entire kidney.
Opposite: N/A
Awkward
Causing or feeling embarrassment or inconvenience.
Navigating small talk with a stranger in a quiet bedroom is deeply awkward.
Opposite: Graceful

💬 Stage 4: Discussion▲

🔵 Round 1: Lyric Analysis & Discussion

In 'Sex with a Stranger', Rebecca's inner monologue is dominated by true crime paranoia. Why do people suspect the worst?
★ Has watching true crime podcasts or shows ever made you unnecessarily suspicious of a stranger?
The song contrasts sultry romantic intentions with the fear of a secret murderer. Why is this juxtaposition hilarious?
★ How do you handle situations where your brain jumps to the most dramatic, dangerous conclusion?
Rebecca constantly worries about whether she is in a safe environment. What precautions do modern daters take?
★ What safety rules or boundaries do you or your friends use when meeting new people?
The lyrics mention urban legends about waking up without a kidney. Why do ridiculous myths persist?
★ What is a funny urban legend or rumor that you secretly worried was real when you were younger?
How does the song capture the extreme awkward tension of casual hookup small talk?
★ How do you break the ice when entering an awkward social or professional situation?
Why does the initial thrill of excitement in dating often turn into overthinking and anxiety?
★ How do you stay present and calm when feeling anxious about a new venture?
Rebecca plans her escape route from the hotel room just in case. Is over-preparing a sign of wisdom or stress?
★ Are you an over-preparer who plans worst-case scenarios, or do you go with the flow?
How does the track explore the difficulty of building real intimacy when trust hasn't been earned yet?
★ What does it take for you to feel genuine trust and intimacy with someone new?
How does the song show that modern true crime media has permanently altered how we perceive casual encounters?
★ Do you think true crime entertainment makes society safer or just more paranoid?
Ultimately, how does Rebecca balance her romantic desires against her wild paranoia?
★ How do you balance being cautious with remaining open to new people and experiences?

🟢 Round 2: Broad Themes & Philosophy Debate

Overconsumption of true crime media has ruined casual social interactions by instilling constant paranoia. Let's debate this perspective.
Taking extreme safety precautions when meeting a stranger is practical common sense, not irrational fear. Share your thoughts.
True emotional intimacy is impossible to achieve in casual dating settings without prior trust. Defend or oppose.
✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Language Focus: Modals of Deduction & Anxiety (*must be, could be*)

In the song Sex with a Stranger, the anxious internal monologue highlights Modals of Speculation & Deduction (e.g., he must be an axe murderer, he could be hiding an axe, he might harvest my kidney). This structure supports expressing dramatic suspicions.

Let's Practise : Three Examples:
  • He must be an escaped murderer because his bathroom is too clean!
  • She could be texting her friends my exact location right now.
  • That strange noise might be a warning sign that I should run.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
She is afraid from the stranger → She is afraid of the stranger (Use 'afraid of' when expressing fear toward someone or something.)
He must to be a murderer → He must be a murderer (Do not use 'to' after modal auxiliary 'must'.)
She wants to feel safely in the room → She wants to feel safe in the room (Use predicate adjective 'safe' after linking verb 'feel'.)
