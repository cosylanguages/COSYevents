📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Insecurity
Uncertainty or anxiety about oneself; lack of confidence.
Paula's gospel anthem encourages Rebecca to ignore basic self-preservation due to insecurity.
Opposite: Confidence
Fear
An unpleasant emotion caused by belief in impending danger.
Facing your fear is good advice, unless your fear is jumping off a cliff without a parachute.
Opposite: Calm
Tiptoe
To walk quietly and carefully with heels raised.
You cannot tiptoe through life if you want to make a bold impact.
Opposite: Stomp
Frightened
Afraid, anxious, or fearful.
She was frightened of public speaking, so she pictured the audience in pajamas.
Opposite: Fearless
Stare
To look fixedly or vacantly at someone or something with eyes wide open.
The song literally advises you to stare directly into a bear's eyes.
Opposite: Glance

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Scary
Causing fear; frightening.
Taking a career risk is scary, but so is staying stuck forever.
Opposite: Comforting
Courage
Bravery and mind strength in the face of pain or grief.
True courage is distinguishing between brave risk-taking and sheer stupidity.
Opposite: Cowardice
Reckless
Without caution; careless of consequences or danger.
Paula's motivational speech crosses the line into hilarious, reckless endangerment.
Opposite: Cautious
Platitude
A flat, dull, or trite remark uttered as if it were fresh or profound.
Self-help books are often full of empty platitudes that ignore reality.
Opposite: Insight
Danger
The possibility of suffering harm or injury.
Ignoring actual danger in the name of confidence is a terrible strategy.
Opposite: Safety

💬 Stage 4: Discussion▲

🔵 Round 1: Lyric Analysis & Discussion

In 'Face Your Fears', Paula sings a gospel choir anthem giving lethal advice like 'run with scissors'. How does it spoof motivational platitudes?
★ What is the worst or most cliché motivational platitude you have ever received?
The song advises ignoring healthy insecurity and jumping off buildings. Why is blind confidence dangerous?
★ How do you distinguish between healthy caution and irrational insecurity?
When Paula urges Rebecca to run straight toward danger, how does the show highlight bad decision-making?
★ Have you ever ignored clear signs of danger because you wanted to prove something?
Why is it bad advice to stare down a literal bear in the woods?
★ What is a hilarious example of advice that sounds inspiring but is completely reckless in practice?
The song tells you never to tiptoe through life. Where is the balance between caution and bold action?
★ In what situation did you stop trying to tiptoe and finally take a bold stand?
Why are people so frightened of failure that they listen to absurd gurus?
★ When you feel frightened about trying something new, how do you calm your mind?
The track turns a scary situation into a triumphant gospel celebration. How does music change perception?
★ What is something that used to feel deeply scary to you that you now handle easily?
How does the song redefine courage by showing that unthinking bravery is just foolishness?
★ What does genuine, thoughtful courage look like in everyday adult life?
Paula urges Rebecca to be completely reckless in her pursuit of love. Why do friends encourage bad choices?
★ Why are we often far more reckless when giving advice to friends than when acting for ourselves?
How does confronting a real fear differ from taking an unnecessary, stupid risk?
★ What is one genuine fear you successfully faced and overcame?

🟢 Round 2: Broad Themes & Philosophy Debate

Most self-help platitudes encourage reckless behavior by confusing basic caution with cowardice. Let's debate this perspective.
True courage lies in knowing when to walk away from danger rather than stubbornly facing every threat. Share your thoughts.
People follow bad advice because the fear of staying stuck outweighs the fear of making a scary mistake. Defend or oppose.
✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Language Focus: Imperatives & Comedic Extreme Directives

In the song Face Your Fears, the parody of gospel advice highlights Direct Imperatives & Hyperbolic Directives (e.g., run with scissors, jump out of the plane, stare at the sun). This structure supports analyzing comedic commands.

Let's Practise : Three Examples:
  • Run directly with scissors toward your ultimate destiny!
  • Stare straight into the bear's eyes and assert your dominance.
  • Jump out of the airplane without checking your parachute!

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
She told him to face to his fears → She told him to face his fears (Do not insert 'to' after the transitive verb 'face'.)
He is frightened from the dark → He is frightened of the dark (Use 'frightened of' or 'frightened by'.)
Running with scissors is a reckless act of danger → Running with scissors is a reckless, dangerous act (Use adjectives in sequence cleanly.)
