📚 Stage 2: Vocabulary▲

❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Greatness
the quality of being great; distinction or eminence.
Striving for artistic greatness requires immense sacrifice.
Opposite: Mediocrity
Haunting
poignant and evocative; difficult to forget.
Her haunting vocal melody stayed in the audience's mind.
Opposite: Forgettable
Surrender
the action of surrendering or yielding to force.
She refused to surrender her vision to corporate pressure.
Opposite: Resistance
Struggle
a forceful effort to overcome obstacles.
The struggle for creative independence defines every artist.
Opposite: Ease
Release
the action of setting free or releasing.
Music provides a cathartic release from inner tension.
Opposite: Restraint

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Echo
a detail that recalls or resembles something else.
Her voice echoed through the ancient stone amphitheater.
Opposite: Silence
Wilderness
an uncultivated, uninhabited, and wild region.
Walking through the creative wilderness can feel lonely.
Opposite: Civilization
Triumph
a great victory or achievement.
Achieving recognition after years of quiet work was a triumph.
Opposite: Defeat
Legacy
something handed down from an ancestor or predecessor.
She wanted to build a musical legacy that inspires future generations.
Opposite: Oblivion
Grace
smoothness and elegance of movement or behavior.
Handling harsh criticism with quiet grace defines true mastery.
Opposite: Clumsiness

💬 Stage 4: Discussion▲

🔵 Round 1: Lyric Analysis & Discussion

In 'one of the greats', Florence Welch reflects on artistic legacy. What defines true greatness in art versus temporary fame?
★ Who is a historical or modern figure you consider a model of true greatness?
The song features a haunting melody that stays with the listener. What makes an artwork or memory deeply haunting?
★ What is a movie or book that left a haunting impression on your mind?
The lyrics describe refusing to surrender to self-doubt. When is holding your ground essential, and when is compromise wise?
★ Have you ever faced a moment where you refused to surrender your core values?
Building a career in art involves ongoing creative struggle. How does struggle refine a person's talent?
★ What personal struggle taught you the most about your own capabilities?
Performing on stage gives the singer a powerful emotional release. What activity gives you a cathartic release of stress?
★ Do you prefer physical exercise or artistic expression for emotional release?
Her voice leaves a lasting echo long after the song ends. What kind of emotional echo do you hope to leave in your work?
★ How do the choices of past generations continue to echo in modern society?
The lyrics evoke wandering through a stormy wilderness. How does spending time in nature or creative wilderness clear your head?
★ Would you enjoy exploring an untouched natural wilderness for a week?
Achieving artistic independence is described as a quiet triumph. What small triumph made you feel proud recently?
★ How do you like to celebrate a major personal triumph with family?
The track contemplates what kind of legacy an artist leaves behind. What kind of legacy do you want to create in your field?
★ Why do people care about leaving a positive legacy for future generations?
Performing under pressure demands composure and grace. How can someone maintain grace under intense public pressure?
★ Who in your life handles difficult moments with extraordinary poise and grace?

🟢 Round 2: Broad Themes & Philosophy Debate

True artistic greatness requires total dedication and sacrifice of personal comfort. Discuss.
Building a long-term legacy is a far more meaningful measure of success than immediate popularity. Defend or oppose.
✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Language Focus: Modal Verb of Obligation (*have to*)

In the song One Of The Greats, the repetitive lyric chunk "To be one of the greats, you have to..." highlights the use of Modal Verb of Obligation (*have to*). This structure supports: Discussing terms of career greatness and legacy.

Let's Practise : Three Examples:
  • Practice using "To be one of the greats, you have to..." or related forms actively during your discussion rounds today.
  • Notice how the lyric chunk "To be one of the greats, you have to..." builds rhythmic familiarity and emotional weight.
  • Incorporate the grammatical structure behind "To be one of the greats, you have to..." in your Stage 6 final speaking challenge.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
To be one of the greats, you have prepare. → To be one of the greats, you have to prepare. (Include 'to' in obligation modal 'have to + verb'.)
She was one of the great in history. → She was one of the greats in history. (Use plural noun 'greats' when referring to distinguished individuals.)
Her voice echoed through the hall. → Her voice echoed across / through the hall. (Preposition 'through' or 'across' accurately describes sound reverberation.)
