❤️ Core Concepts & Feelings

Consumerism
the protection or promotion of the interests of consumers.
The song critiques aggressive consumerism and corporate influence.
Opposite: Minimalism
Anxiety
a feeling of worry, nervousness, or unease.
Living in a fast-paced culture can trigger deep anxiety.
Opposite: Calmness
Assimilation
the process of taking in and fully understanding culture.
Cultural assimilation can strip away local identities.
Opposite: Preservation
Paranoia
unjustified suspicion and mistrust of others.
Media sensationalism feeds public paranoia and fear.
Opposite: Trust
Isolation
the state of being isolated or alone.
Surrounded by loud advertisements, he felt total isolation.
Opposite: Belonging

🗣️ Life, Actions & Connection

Superficiality
lack of thoroughness, depth, or character.
The character Johnny represents commercial superficiality.
Opposite: Depth
Vulnerability
the quality or state of being exposed to harm.
Admitting fear exposes our inner vulnerability.
Opposite: Invulnerability
Obsession
the state of being obsessed with someone or something.
The public obsession with celebrity culture distorts reality.
Opposite: Indifference
Influence
the capacity to have an effect on character or development.
Foreign media exerts a powerful influence on young minds.
Opposite: Powerlessness
Globalization
the process by which businesses develop international influence.
Globalization spreads fast food and pop culture everywhere.
Opposite: Localism

💬 Stage 4: Discussion▲

🔵 Round 1: Lyric Analysis & Discussion

In 'I'm Afraid of Americans', Bowie sings 'Johnny's in America'. How does commercial consumerism shape global lifestyle choices?
★ How do advertisements influence what you choose to buy in your daily life?
The repeated line 'I'm afraid of Americans' conveys intense social anxiety. What causes cultural anxiety in today's world?
★ How do you manage feelings of anxiety when entering an unfamiliar social setting?
Bowie observes foreign culture taking over everywhere. Is rapid cultural assimilation enriching or damaging?
★ How well does your city preserve its local heritage against global cultural assimilation?
The music video depicts Bowie being chased through city streets, evoking paranoia. What fuels modern urban paranoia?
★ Do social media headlines make you feel cautious or feed unnecessary paranoia?
Despite being surrounded by crowds and media, the protagonist experiences deep isolation. How can big cities foster isolation?
★ What do you do to stay connected and avoid isolation when living in a busy town?
The character 'Johnny' buys stars and plans without deeper meaning, showing superficiality. Why is superficiality so common in pop culture?
★ How do you distinguish between genuine friendship and surface-level superficiality?
Repeating 'I'm afraid' reveals a rare male vulnerability. Is showing vulnerability a sign of weakness or courage?
★ In what situations do you find it easiest to share your true vulnerability?
The character's endless desire to buy God and land highlights a corporate obsession. What is a modern social obsession that concerns you?
★ Have you ever developed a harmless personal obsession with a hobby or song?
American pop media exerts enormous global influence. Is cultural influence always a one-way street?
★ Who is a public figure whose cultural influence you admire or question?
Bowie's track critiques the dark side of cultural globalization. What is one positive and one negative effect of globalization?
★ Do you enjoy having access to global products, or do you prefer local craft?

🟢 Round 2: Broad Themes & Philosophy Debate

Global commercial consumerism erodes local traditions and creates widespread psychological anxiety. Discuss.
Expressing fear of dominant foreign cultural influence is a legitimate defense of local identity rather than xenophobia. Defend or oppose.
✨ Stage 5: Language Focus▲

Language Focus: Adjective + Preposition Collocations (*afraid of*)

In the song Im Afraid Of Americans, the repetitive lyric chunk "I'm afraid of Americans, I'm afraid of..." highlights the use of Adjective + Preposition Collocations (*afraid of*). This structure supports: Exploring cultural hegemony and paranoia.

Let's Practise : Three Examples:
  • Practice using "I'm afraid of Americans, I'm afraid of..." or related forms actively during your discussion rounds today.
  • Notice how the lyric chunk "I'm afraid of Americans, I'm afraid of..." builds rhythmic familiarity and emotional weight.
  • Incorporate the grammatical structure behind "I'm afraid of Americans, I'm afraid of..." in your Stage 6 final speaking challenge.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I am afraid from Americans. → I am afraid of Americans. (Use adjective + preposition collocation 'afraid of'.)
Johnny is wanting to buy a plan. → Johnny wants to buy a plan. (Stative verbs like 'want' are rarely used in continuous tenses.)
He is scared about the future. → He is scared of the future. (Use 'scared of' when naming the source of fear.)
