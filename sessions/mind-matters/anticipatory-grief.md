vocabulary:
  - word: "Anticipatory grief"
    definition: "the emotional rehearsal of loss that occurs before the actual separation or death takes place."
    example: "Caregivers often experience intense anticipatory grief, mourning the living long before they are gone."
  - word: "Terminal"
    definition: "predicted to lead to death, especially slowly; incurable."
    example: "The sudden reality of a terminal diagnosis changes the psychological timeline of the entire family."
  - word: "Pre-emptive"
    definition: "done to forestall or prepare for a future event, especially to shield oneself from trauma."
    example: "Her detachment was a pre-emptive defense mechanism designed to soften the coming blow of separation."
  - word: "Somatic"
    definition: "relating to or affecting the body as distinct from the mind; physical manifestation of emotion."
    example: "Deep emotional distress often translates into somatic symptoms like chronic fatigue and chest tightness."
  - word: "Bereavement"
    definition: "the period of grief and mourning after the death or permanent departure of a loved one."
    example: "Understanding anticipatory mourning can make the subsequent bereavement period less disorienting."
  - word: "Resilience"
    definition: "the capacity to recover from or adapt to difficult life transitions and psychological stress."
    example: "True emotional resilience is not about feeling no pain, but about integrating grief into one's life story."
  - word: "Compassion fatigue"
    definition: "physical and emotional exhaustion resulting from a prolonged exposure to others' trauma and caregiving."
    example: "Without self-directed empathy, the primary caregiver fell victim to severe compassion fatigue."
  - word: "Existential"
    definition: "relating to human existence, meaning, and the inevitable transience of life."
    example: "Facing the loss of a parent triggers a profound existential crisis, forcing us to question our own purpose."
  - word: "Groundedness"
    definition: "the state of being sensible, realistic, and psychologically connected to the present reality."
    example: "Mindfulness practices can help caregivers restore a sense of groundedness amidst chaotic hospital environments."
  - word: "Transience"
    definition: "the state of lasting only for a short time; impermanence."
    example: "Accepting the absolute transience of beautiful moments allows us to cherish them without clinging."

mistakes:
  - wrong: "I am mourning about him"
    right: "I am mourning him / I am in mourning for him"
    note: "(Preposition usage: the verb 'mourn' is transitive and does not require a preposition)"
  - wrong: "He is inevitable to fail"
    right: "It is inevitable that he will fail / His failure is inevitable"
    note: "(Structural error: we use 'It is inevitable that' or make 'failure' the subject)"
  - wrong: "I have many compassion for her"
    right: "I have a lot of compassion / great compassion for her"
    note: "(Quantifiers with uncountable nouns: use 'a lot of' or 'great' instead of 'many')"
  - wrong: "I was sympathizing with her struggles"
    right: "I was empathizing with her struggles / showed deep empathy"
    note: "('Sympathize' implies simple pity, while 'empathize' denotes deep psychological understanding)"
