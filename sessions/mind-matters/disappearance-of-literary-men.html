Duration

60 minutes

Languages

🇬🇧 English

Level

Intermediate (B1)

Theme

Reading Habits, Gender, & Empathy

Based on cultural research regarding reading trends (NYT Opinion). Explore why men are reading fewer fiction novels, how literature builds empathy, and the impact of digital media.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Literature ≠ triviality
written works, especially those considered of superior or lasting artistic merit.
Reading classic literature expands emotional understanding.
Fiction ≠ fact
literature in the form of prose, especially short stories and novels, describing imaginary events.
Fiction allows readers to inhabit different lives.
Decline ≠ growth
a gradual and continuous loss of strength, numbers, quality, or value.
Statistics show a decline in male fiction readers.
Empathy ≠ indifference
the ability to understand and share the feelings of another.
Reading novels builds deep interpersonal empathy.
Narrative ≠ list
a spoken or written account of connected events; a story.
A compelling narrative engages both heart and intellect.
Insight ≠ ignorance
the capacity to gain an accurate and deep intuitive understanding of a person or thing.
Great books offer profound psychological insight.
Habit ≠ anomaly
a settled or regular tendency or practice.
Daily reading habits have shifted toward short videos.
Engagement ≠ distraction
the state of being involved and focused.
Deep narrative engagement requires sustained attention.
Perspective ≠ bias
a particular attitude toward or way of regarding something; a point of view.
Literature exposes us to diverse cultural perspectives.
Reflection ≠ impulse
serious thought or consideration.
Reading requires quiet time for personal reflection.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • What comes to your mind when you hear about this topic?
  • How does this subject relate to your daily habits?
⚡ Grammar Practice : Present Perfect Continuous for Unfinished Trends (B1)▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Intermediate (B1))

Use has/have been + verb-ing to describe ongoing trends that started in the past (e.g., Men have been reading fewer novels over recent years).

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Men are reading less books since ten years. → Men have been reading fewer books for ten years. (Use present perfect continuous + 'for' for duration up to present.)
Fiction books gives us empathies. → Fiction books give us empathy. (Empathy is an uncountable abstract noun: use 'empathy'.)
