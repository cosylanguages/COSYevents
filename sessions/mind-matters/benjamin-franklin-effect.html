Intermediate (B1)

Topic

Psychology of relationships and the phenomenon of liking those we help.

This session deconstructs the counter-intuitive Benjamin Franklin Effect-the psychological mechanism where performing a favor for someone actually increases our affection for them. We will examine how the brain resolves cognitive dissonance by reframing our social preferences to align with our investments of effort.

🧠 Subconscious Mind Profile

Core Human Tendency Consistency of Self-Image
Subconscious Trigger Performing Small Favors for Others
Psychological Phenomenon Cognitive Dissonance Resolution
Self-Reflection Anchor Rationalizing Interpersonal Relationships

📖 Session Vocabulary

Interpersonal chemistry
the psychological and emotional connection that develops between individuals.
They mistook their shared project struggles for a rare, organic interpersonal chemistry.
Cognitive dissonance
the mental discomfort that occurs when a person's behavior contradicts their beliefs.
Asking an adversary for a favor triggers cognitive dissonance, forcing them to re-evaluate their dislike.
Self-perception theory
the psychological model suggesting we determine our attitudes by observing our own behavior.
According to self-perception theory, we conclude we like someone because we chose to help them.
Reciprocity
the social norm of exchanging actions, kindness, or benefits for mutual advantage.
True connection transcends simple reciprocity; it reshapes our underlying emotional alignments.
Adversary
an opponent, competitor, or person who opposes or is hostile toward another.
Franklin successfully transformed a bitter political adversary into a lifelong, cooperative ally.
Attitude
a settled way of thinking or feeling about someone, typically reflected in behavior.
His hostile attitude softened immediately after he agreed to lend his rare book.
Favor
an act of kindness or helpfulness beyond what is strictly required or expected.
Lending the rare book was a strategic favor designed to resolve political tension.
Connection
an emotional or intellectual relationship characterized by deep mutual understanding.
A small, cooperative gesture can establish a profound, lasting connection between complete strangers.
Indebtedness
the state of feeling obliged or duty-bound to return a benefit or kindness.
He disliked the feeling of social indebtedness, preferring to keep his relationships strictly transactional.
Cooperate
to work or act together with others to achieve shared goals or mutual benefits.
When forced to cooperate on a crisis, rivals often discover their shared humanity and values.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up ▲
The "Benjamin Franklin Effect" says that doing a favor for someone makes you like them more.
  • Do we help people because we like them, or do we like them because we have helped them?
  • Is our affection for other people just a story we tell ourselves to justify our actions?
🔵 Round 1 : Deep Discussion ▲
Questions
10 items required. Use .round-item-main and .round-item-personal.
How does the brain utilize Interpersonal chemistry as a convenient cover for simple self-justification?
★ Have you ever felt a sudden spark of Interpersonal chemistry that you later realized was purely situational?
Why does performing a favor for an Adversary trigger intense Cognitive dissonance in their mind?
★ When was the last time you experienced severe Cognitive dissonance after helping someone you didn't respect?
How does Self-perception theory challenge the romantic notion that our likes and dislikes are deeply authentic?
★ Do you determine your own beliefs by analyzing your past behavior, or do you feel they are innate?
Is social Reciprocity a universal human instinct, or is it a calculated survival mechanism?
★ How do you react when someone violates the unwritten rules of social Reciprocity in your network?
Why is it psychologically easier to ask an Adversary for help than to offer them a direct gift?
★ Have you ever swallowed your pride to ask a professional Adversary for technical assistance?
To what extent can we consciously reprogram our hostile Attitude toward a colleague through small shared tasks?
★ What is the hardest part about maintaining a neutral, professional Attitude in a toxic workplace?
What distinguishes a genuine Favor from a calculated, manipulative transaction in corporate networking?
★ Can you recall a time you did a substantial Favor for someone who never acknowledged your effort?
How do simple, shared activities slowly build a secure, psychological Connection between strangers?
★ Where do you feel the most secure emotional Connection in your current professional circle?
Why does a sense of social Indebtedness often make us feel uncomfortable and defensive?
★ Have you ever avoided a colleague because you felt an overwhelming sense of Indebtedness toward them?
Do people automatically learn to respect rivals when they are forced to Cooperate under high stress?
★ How do you motivate yourself to Cooperate with someone whose personality you deeply dislike?
🟣 Let's Speak Together ▲

The Chemistry Formula: Imagine you are a negotiator trying to reconcile two bitter corporate founders. Design a strategy to build a connection using at least one second conditional, one third conditional, and one mixed conditional.

🟢 Round 2 : Conditional Scenarios ▲
Dilemmas & Projections
10 items required. Speculate, brainstorm, and share your personal opinions and ideas.
If you asked your rival for a small Favor tomorrow, would they secretly begin to respect you more?
If modern society banned all transactional favors, do you think we would still experience social Reciprocity ?
If your brain automatically resolved all Cognitive dissonance , would you lose your capacity for self-reflection?
If you had applied Self-perception theory to your last conflict, would you have avoided that bitter argument?
If they had chosen to Cooperate back then, would their company have achieved industry dominance today?
If your past partner had shown true Sincerity in their actions, would you still be together today?
If you had not felt that subtle sense of Indebtedness , would you have quit that high-pressure job much sooner?
If you had never developed that unique Connection with your mentor, where would you be working today?
If their toxic Attitude had not been addressed by HR last year, would you still be with the company today?
If you had known the psychology of the Franklin effect, would you have transformed that Adversary into an ally?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections) ▲
I ask to him a favor → I ask him a favor (Ask someone something)
It depends of the situation → It depends on the situation (Depends on)
I'm more social than him → I'm more social than he is (Comparison)
He persuaded to me → He persuaded me (Direct object)
I was sympathizing with her struggles → I was empathizing with her struggles / showed deep empathy (Linguistic register: 'sympathize' suggests feeling pity, while 'empathize' implies a deeper psychological understanding and shared feelings)
