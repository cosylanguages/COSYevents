Psychology, Tech, & Attention Economy

Based on Psychology Today analysis of Yanis Varoufakis's Technofeudalism theory. Explore platform monopolies, surveillance, attention harvesting, and reclaiming mental autonomy.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Feudalism ≠ democracy
a social system based on landholding and vassal duty.
Technofeudalism describes digital platforms acting as digital landlords.
Attention economy ≠ free exchange
the perspective treating human attention as a scarce commodity.
Social media platforms trade in the attention economy.
Platform ≠ independent site
a digital service providing a foundation for application hosting.
Tech platforms rent digital space to creators.
Monopolize ≠ share
to obtain exclusive possession or control of a market.
Big tech firms monopolize user attention data.
Exploitation ≠ fairness
the action of treating someone unfairly to benefit from their work.
Constant notifications lead to emotional exploitation.
Engagement ≠ disconnection
the state of being actively involved with digital media.
Algorithms prioritize high outrage for maximum engagement.
Surveillance ≠ privacy
close observation, especially of a suspected spy or user.
Surveillance capitalism tracks online behavior.
Addiction ≠ moderation
the state of being enslaved to a habit or practice.
Infinite scrolling features trigger device addiction.
Vassal ≠ sovereign
a person or entity in a subordinate position.
Users act like digital vassals generating data for platform owners.
Reclaim ≠ surrender
to retrieve or recover something lost.
We must reclaim control over our daily attention span.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Data is collect by apps. → Data is collected by apps. (Passive voice requires past participle 'collected'.)
I pay attention on my screen. → I pay attention to my screen. (Preposition rule: 'pay attention to'.)
