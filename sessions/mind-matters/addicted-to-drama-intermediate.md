vocabulary:
  - word: "Adrenaline"
    definition: "a hormone released during stress, increasing heart rate and energy."
    example: "High-conflict arguments trigger a sudden rush of physical adrenaline in our bodies."
  - word: "Addiction"
    definition: "the state of being enslaved to a habit or psychological dependency."
    example: "Many psychologists describe drama-seeking behavior as a form of emotional addiction."
  - word: "Chaos"
    definition: "complete disorder and confusion."
    example: "Some people unconsciously create chaos in their relationships because peace feels boring to them."
  - word: "Validation"
    definition: "recognition or affirmation that a person's feelings are worthwhile."
    example: "Seeking validation through constant complaints is a common but unhealthy social habit."
  - word: "Conflict"
    definition: "a serious disagreement, argument, or clash."
    example: "Unresolved childhood wounds can make us repeat the same relationship conflict as adults."
  - word: "Subconscious"
    definition: "relating to the part of the mind we are not fully aware of."
    example: "Our subconscious mind often seeks familiar patterns, even if those patterns are painful."
  - word: "Instability"
    definition: "the state of being unstable or lacking steady peace."
    example: "Growing up in household instability can condition a child to expect constant crisis."
  - word: "Provoke"
    definition: "to stimulate or deliberately trigger a strong, angry reaction."
    example: "It is unhelpful to provoke an argument just to test your partner's loyalty."
  - word: "Boredom"
    definition: "feeling weary and restless through lack of active interest."
    example: "To escape quiet boredom, some individuals initiate gossip or dramatic rumors."
  - word: "Defense mechanism"
    definition: "an automatic mental reaction used to protect oneself from anxiety."
    example: "Blaming others for your mistakes is a primitive defense mechanism that prevents growth."

mistakes:

    right: 'If I <strong>were</strong> you, I would stop fighting.'
    note: '(Underlined error: in the ''if'' clause of a second conditional, use the past subjunctive ''were'' instead of ''would be'')'
  - wrong: 'She is addicted <u>on drama</u>.'
    right: 'She is addicted <strong>to drama</strong>.'
    note: '(Underlined prepositional error. Correct collocation: ''addicted to'')'
  - wrong: 'The chaos made me <u>to feel anxious</u>.'
    right: 'The chaos made me <strong>feel anxious</strong>.'
    note: '(Underlined error: causative verb ''make'' takes a bare infinitive without ''to'')'
