This session celebrates World Nature Conservation Day, a global event dedicated to protecting our natural resources and wild habitats. We discuss the importance of preserving biodiversity, saving water, and adopting daily sustainable habits. The lesson provides useful expressions to talk about environmental challenges and sharing social responsibilities.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Conservation
the action of protecting and conserving the natural environment.
Nature conservation is necessary to guarantee clean drinking water for the future.
Ecosystem
a biological community of interacting organisms and their environment.
A healthy forest ecosystem contains thousands of insects, birds, and tall trees.
Biodiversity
the variety of plant and animal life in a particular habitat.
Building concrete highways reduces local biodiversity and threatens wild animals.
Sustainability
the ability to maintain balance without depleting natural resources.
Local agricultural sustainability depends heavily on responsible irrigation habits.
Preserving
maintaining something in its original, safe state.
Preserving our ancient forests is a fundamental duty for modern governments.
Endangered
seriously at risk of extinction.
The Siberian tiger is a highly endangered species protected by international laws.
Habitat
the natural home or environment of an animal or plant.
Deforestation destroys the natural habitat of wild birds and monkeys.
Pollution
the presence of harmful or poisonous substances in the environment.
Plastic pollution in the oceans causes severe damage to seafaring turtles.
Restoration
the action of returning something to its original healthy condition.
The ecological restoration of the local lake took three years of active cleanup.
Responsibility
the state or fact of having a duty to deal with something.
It is our collective responsibility to keep our city parks clean and tidy.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
  • What is the most beautiful natural place or national park you have ever visited?
  • What small environmental habit do you practice every single day?
🔵 Round 1 : General Discussion▲
Questions
What is the primary goal of World Nature conservation, and why was this global day established?
※ Have you ever participated in a voluntary tree planting or park cleanup event?
Why is every animal and plant species essential to maintain a stable ecosystem?
※ What wild animals or birds live in the green areas near your home?
How does global warming affect biological biodiversity in cold mountain environments?
※ Do you prefer spending your holidays in high mountains or near the ocean?
What does agricultural sustainability look like in countries with very limited rain?
※ Do you try to save tap water when washing dishes or taking a shower?
Why is preserving historical rivers and lakes so critical for nearby cities?
※ Is the river or lake in your hometown clean enough for swimming?
What is the main biological threat facing an endangered mammal species?
※ What is your favorite wild animal, and is it protected by conservation laws?
How can urban development be planned to protect wild habitat corridors?
※ Does your city have enough parks, botanical gardens, and green spaces?
How does chemical and industrial pollution alter the temperature of our atmosphere?
※ Do you think air pollution in your city is getting better or worse?
What is required to achieve complete ecological restoration after a major forest fire?
※ Have you ever witnessed a major natural disaster or extreme weather event?
Why is protecting natural resources a shared global responsibility rather than a local one?
※ Who do you think should pay for clean energy: taxpayers or private companies?

🟢 Round 2 : Agree or Disagree: The Future▲
Predictions
In tomorrow's world, will global conservation efforts be governed by AI satellite networks instead of human rangers?
※ Would you trust an automated drone to protect national park boundaries?
Will every urban ecosystem be artificial, with vertical green walls replacing real ground grass?
※ Do you think vertical farming can completely replace traditional horizontal fields?
As we colonize other planets, will earthly biodiversity be permanently preserved only as stored genetic databases?
※ If you could travel to a colony on Mars, would you go, or would you stay on Earth?
Will the concept of environmental sustainability become obsolete because of synthetic clean energy breakthroughs?
※ Do you believe cheap unlimited fusion energy will solve all pollution problems?
Will preserving pristine wild rivers become a luxurious tourist privilege reserved only for the wealthy?
※ Should access to wild national parks always remain completely free for everyone?
Will we clone and restore every endangered mammal back to health using advanced DNA editing techniques?
※ Would you support cloning a woolly mammoth to release it in modern cold forests?
Will wild animal habitat areas expand dramatically as human populations move into compact, high-rise smart cities?
※ Would you prefer to live in a highly dense vertical city to let nature recover around it?
Will plastic and oil-based pollution be completely eliminated by biodegradable vegetable packaging?
※ Do you try to avoid buying items wrapped in multiple layers of plastic?
Will the restoration of damaged oceans require hundreds of years of international cooperative funding?
※ Do you think countries should work closer together to protect international waters?
Will individual responsibility be replaced by automatic carbon taxes tracked directly through credit card purchases?
※ Would you accept a card that limited your purchases based on your personal carbon budget?
✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
We must protect the natures. → We must protect nature. (Underlined error: 'nature' is an uncountable noun and does not have a plural form when referring to the environment)
This species is in danger of extinction. → This species is endangered / in danger of extinction. (Both forms are correct! 'Endangered' is a highly efficient adjective synonym)
The forest was destructed by human activity. → The forest was destroyed by / devastated by human activity. (Underlined error: the past participle of 'destroy' is 'destroyed', not 'destructed')
