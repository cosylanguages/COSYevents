📖 Session Vocabulary

Peak ≠ valley
the pointed top of a mountain.
Reaching the mountain peak required hours of steep climbing.
Altitude ≠ sea level
the height of an object or point in relation to sea level.
High altitude makes breathing much more difficult.
Expedition ≠ routine
a journey undertaken by a group of people with a particular purpose.
Mountaineers prepared for a three-week Arctic expedition.
Wilderness ≠ city
an uncultivated, uninhabited, and natural region.
Walking in the mountain wilderness brings inner calm.
Endurance ≠ weakness
the ability to withstand hardship or adversity.
Climbing steep slopes tests your physical endurance.
Biodiversity ≠ monotony
the variety of plant and animal life in a habitat.
Mountain ecosystems harbor rich plant biodiversity.
Terrain ≠ flat ground
a stretch of land, especially with regard to its physical features.
Rocky terrain demands sturdy hiking footwear.
Conservation ≠ destruction
preservation and protection of natural environments.
Mountain conservation protects vital freshwater sources.
Tranquility ≠ noise
the quality or state of being tranquil; calm.
The tranquility of mountain lakes attracts many visitors.
Summit ≠ base
the highest point of a hill or mountain.
They celebrated after reaching the summit at sunrise.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Modals of Ability in the Past (Could / Managed to) (B1)▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Intermediate (B1))

Use could for general past ability and managed to + verb for specific difficult achievements (e.g., We managed to reach the peak despite the storm).

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Yesterday we could reach the summit despite the storm. → Yesterday we managed to reach the summit despite the storm. (For a single past success against difficulty, use 'managed to', not 'could'.)
The mountain air is more clean. → The mountain air is cleaner. (Short adjective comparative form takes '-er': cleaner.)
