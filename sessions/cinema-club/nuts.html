🎬 Session Vocabulary

1. Generic Discussion Vocabulary

competency hearing
a formal court proceeding to determine whether a defendant is mentally fit to stand trial.
Claudia Dempsey demands a competency hearing to prove she is sane enough to face manslaughter charges.
institutional control
the power exerted by psychiatric and legal authorities over an individual's body and freedom.
Claudia's wealthy parents attempt to force institutional control upon her to avoid a public trial and scandal.
legal autonomy
the legal right of an individual to make their own decisions and defend themselves in court.
Claudia fights fiercely for her legal autonomy, refusing to allow her attorney to enter a plea of insanity.
paternal betrayal
the painful realization that a father figure has exploited or abused a child's trust.
Courtroom testimony reveals a history of paternal betrayal that drove Claudia to escape her affluent family.
courtroom defense
the legal strategy and arguments presented by an attorney to represent a defendant.
Public defender Aaron Levinsky builds a passionate courtroom defense arguing that Claudia's anger is a rational response to abuse.

2. Authentic Film Quotes & Slang

I'm not crazy
(signature character line) Claudia's persistent assertion that being angry and rebellious is not the same as being insane.
Claudia shouts "I'm not crazy!" at the judge, insisting that being declared incompetent steals her right to a fair trial.
manslaughter charge
(legal charge) the felony allegation Claudia faces after killing a violent client in self-defense.
If declared mentally incompetent, Claudia will be locked in an asylum indefinitely without ever fighting her manslaughter charge.
Bellevue psychiatric
(proper noun / setting) the famous New York hospital ward where Claudia is held for psychiatric evaluation.
Claudia resists the restraints and sedatives administered by doctors inside Bellevue psychiatric ward.
Aaron Levinsky
(proper noun / character) the dedicated court-appointed public defender who agrees to respect Claudia's wishes.
Aaron Levinsky overcomes Claudia's initial distrust, working tirelessly to demonstrate her mental competency.
five minutes of sanity
(pivotal speech motif) Claudia's emotional argument that she deserves her day in court regardless of her background.
Claudia challenges the psychiatrists, questioning whether her wealthy parents' desire to hide the truth defines her sanity.

Screenplay Discussion
Deconstruct the character conversations and screenplay dynamics. Integrate Subjunctive Mood in Courtroom Demands into your analysis.
Claudia Dempsey demands a formal competency hearing to prove she is mentally fit to stand trial for manslaughter. Why does she refuse to enter an insanity plea?
★ Why is having the legal right to speak for yourself and defend your actions essential to human dignity?
Her wealthy parents attempt to force institutional control upon her, preferring an asylum commitment to public embarrassment. How does Claudia fight their authority?
★ How do families sometimes misuse authority or money to control relatives and protect their own reputation?
Claudia fights fiercely to maintain her legal autonomy in court. How does her determination challenge the psychiatric experts testifying against her?
★ What does it take to stand up against powerful institutions when everyone around you doubts your sanity?
Courtroom testimony exposes a horrific history of paternal betrayal and childhood abuse. How does bringing these secrets to light change the judge's perception?
★ Why is breaking silence about past trauma a crucial step toward achieving personal justice?
Public defender Aaron Levinsky builds an aggressive courtroom defense, arguing that Claudia's explosive anger is a rational reaction to systemic abuse. Is his argument compelling?
★ Can intense anger and rebellion be a healthy, rational response to living in an abusive environment?
Claudia repeatedly insists 'I'm not crazy' during her outbursts in the courtroom. How does her passion demonstrate her sharp mind despite her erratic behavior?
★ How do you distinguish between someone who is genuinely unstable and someone who is expressing righteous anger?
Claudia faces a serious manslaughter charge after defending herself against a violent client in an apartment. How does her past as a high-priced escort affect the trial?
★ How do social prejudices against a person's profession or lifestyle bias the legal system against them?
While held in the bellevue psychiatric ward, Claudia resists forced medication and restraints. How does the hospital environment mirror a prison?
★ What measures should be in place to protect the rights of patients in psychiatric facilities?
Court-appointed attorney aaron levinsky gradually earns Claudia's trust by listening to her wishes instead of condescending to her. What makes a great legal advocate?
★ What qualities make an advocate, mentor, or lawyer truly trustworthy when representing someone in trouble?
Claudia delivers a powerful speech demanding 'five minutes of sanity', arguing that the court cares more about legal convenience than her truth. How does Barbra Streisand deliver this climax?
★ Why is having an opportunity to tell your story in your own words so vital for personal healing?
🟣 Cinema Spotlight : Scene Study & Adaptation▲

Compare the adaptation differences and visual contrasts in this film.

Stage Play vs. Screenplay Adaptations
• Adapted from Tom Topor's 1979 Off-Broadway play, maintaining the play's tight, dialogue-driven courtroom structure.
• Barbra Streisand acquired the film rights herself and hired Martin Ritt (Norma Rae) to direct the gritty judicial drama.
Visual Directing & Courtroom Staging
• Confined, sterile interiors inside Bellevue Hospital and a wood-paneled New York courtroom create an atmosphere of institutional pressure.
• Tight close-up camera angles focus intensely on facial expressions during high-stakes cross-examinations.
🟢 Part 2 : Artistry, Interpretation & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Artistic Analysis
Analyze the cinematography, visual motifs, directorial choices, and narrative themes. Integrate Subjunctive Mood in Courtroom Demands into your responses.
Director Martin Ritt sets almost the entire film inside a cramped, sterile courtroom and hospital rooms. How does this claustrophobic staging amplify the emotional intensity?
★ How do single-location or courtroom settings heighten dramatic focus on dialogue and acting performance?
Barbra Streisand produced, scored, and starred in Nuts, delivering a raw, unglamorous dramatic performance. How does her portrayal of Claudia contrast with her famous musical roles?
★ Do you enjoy seeing famous musical or comedy performers tackle dark, intense dramatic roles?
Richard Dreyfuss plays public defender Aaron Levinsky with passionate, fast-talking energy. How does his chemistry with Streisand drive the courtroom battle?
★ What makes a courtroom battle between defense and prosecution captivating to watch on screen?
Karl Malden plays Claudia's stepfather, Arthur, projecting a respectable facade that hides a dark history of abuse. How does the film reveal his hypocrisy during cross-examination?
★ Why are villains who project outwardly respectable, moral personas often the most terrifying?
The film explores how psychiatric diagnoses can sometimes be weaponized to silence inconvenient people. How relevant is this critique to modern legal and medical systems?
★ How can society ensure that medical diagnoses are used to help people rather than strip away their civil rights?
Streisand composed the film's original instrumental score, using subtle piano melodies during traumatic recollections. How does her music support the emotional drama without becoming melodramatic?
★ How can a quiet, understated musical score heighten the impact of intense dramatic scenes?
The intense cross-examination of the court psychiatrist, Dr. Herbert Morrison, exposes how superficial evaluations can misjudge a person. How effective was this legal strategy?
★ How should expert witnesses be questioned in court when their opinions determine a person's freedom?
Claudia's mother, played by Maureen Stapleton, is torn between protecting her husband's reputation and telling the truth about her daughter. Was her ultimate confession in court earned?
★ Why is it so difficult for family members to acknowledge and speak out against abuse happening inside their own home?
The judge eventually rules that Claudia is competent to stand trial, giving her the right to face a jury. Why is this legal victory so triumphant despite the upcoming criminal trial?
★ Why is gaining the right to fight for yourself often a bigger victory than an easy compromise?
If you were directing a modern courtroom drama about mental health and legal rights, what contemporary issues regarding personal autonomy would you highlight?
★ What personal freedoms and legal rights do you believe are most important to defend in today's courtrooms?

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
He insisted she goes to Runway→He insisted she go to RunwayVerbs of demand/insistence like 'insist' trigger the subjunctive/bare infinitive.
I'm used to watch movies in original→I'm used to watching movies in originalThe phrase 'be used to' is followed by a gerund (-ing form), not a bare infinitive.
Seldom I have seen such an ending→Seldom have I seen such an endingNegative adverbs like 'seldom' at the beginning of a clause trigger subject-auxiliary inversion.
