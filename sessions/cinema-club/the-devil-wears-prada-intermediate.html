🎬 Session Vocabulary

1. Generic Discussion Vocabulary

workplace hierarchy
a system in an organization where employees are ranked according to authority and status.
Andy quickly realizes that Runway magazine operates under a rigid workplace hierarchy with Miranda Priestly at the very top.
professional ambition
the strong desire and determination to achieve success and advance in one's career.
As Andy's professional ambition grows, her personal relationships with her boyfriend and friends begin to suffer.
editorial prestige
the high reputation, influence, and respect associated with leading a major publication.
Runway magazine commands immense editorial prestige, making a job there a dream for aspiring fashion journalists.
corporate conformity
the practice of matching one's appearance, behavior, and values to the expectations of a company.
To survive under Miranda, Andy surrenders her casual style and embraces complete corporate conformity.
personal compromise
the act of giving up one's personal values, standards, or relationships to reach a goal.
Andy faces a moral dilemma when she sees the steep personal compromise required to secure her position in Paris.

2. Authentic Film Quotes & Slang

gird your loins
(idiom) prepare oneself mentally or physically for a difficult, stressful, or demanding situation.
Nigel shouts "Gird your loins!" across the Runway office whenever Miranda Priestly unexpectedly enters the building.
clackers
(slang) a derogatory nickname used by Runway staff for style-obsessed assistants whose high heels clack against office floors.
Nigel playfully warns Andy not to turn into one of the mindless "clackers" who obsess over designer shoes.
groundbreaking
(ironic quote / adjective) innovative and pioneering, used sarcastically in the film to mock cliché ideas.
Miranda dryly dismisses a springtime floral concept with her famous line, "Florals? For spring? Groundbreaking."
that's all
(signature catchphrase) a cold, abrupt phrase used to dismiss someone and end a conversation immediately.
Miranda concludes every intense meeting or task delivery with a quiet, terrifying "That's all."
a million girls would kill for this job
(key dialogue quote) a phrase highlighting the extreme competitiveness and prestige of working at Runway.
Emily repeatedly reminds Andy that "a million girls would kill for this job" whenever Andy complains about Miranda's demands.

🔵 Part 1 : Dialogue, Themes & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Screenplay Discussion
Deconstruct the character conversations and screenplay dynamics. Integrate Imperatives & Softened Directives into your analysis.
When Andy Sachs first enters Runway's offices, Nigel warns her about the strict workplace hierarchy. How should a new assistant navigate Miranda's demands-perhaps try arriving before anyone else and keeping excuses to a minimum?
★ If you found yourself working under an intimidating supervisor like Miranda Priestly, how would you protect your mental wellbeing?
Nigel famously shouts 'gird your loins!' when Miranda arrives unexpectedly. What softened directives would you offer Andy on her first day-such as be sure to memorize the book preview before six o'clock?
★ Have you ever experienced a high-stress workplace where everyone scrambled to prepare for a boss's arrival?
Andy insists that her professional ambition lies in investigative journalism rather than fashion. At what point in the film does her focus begin to shift toward succeeding at Runway?
★ Is it better to stick strictly to your original career plan or adapt when unexpected opportunities arise?
Emily belittles Andy for not recognizing famous designers, treating her as an outsider among the fashion-obsessed clackers. Why do the Runway staff place so much value on outward appearance?
★ How do dress codes and office aesthetics influence how people are judged in professional environments?
Runway commands immense global editorial prestige, dictating department store collections months in advance. How does Miranda's cerulean sweater explanation illustrate this hidden economic influence?
★ Were you surprised by Miranda's explanation of how high fashion decisions trickle down to everyday clothing?
When Miranda dryly dismisses spring floral proposals with 'groundbreaking', what does her sarcasm reveal about the relentless pressure to innovate in fashion media?
★ How do you react when someone uses subtle sarcasm to critique your work or ideas?
As Andy succumbs to corporate conformity, her personal relationships with Nate and her old friends begin to strain. What warnings would you give her about losing her identity?
★ Have you ever had to balance demanding job expectations with personal relationships?
Whenever Miranda finishes delivering a list of complex demands, she concludes with a cold 'that's all'. How does this simple phrase enforce her absolute authority over her staff?
★ Why is quiet, calm authority often more intimidating in leadership than loud yelling?
Andy faces a painful personal compromise when Miranda orders her to take Emily's place at the Paris fashion week. How would you phrase advice to Andy about making such a tough decision?
★ If accepting a major career promotion meant disappointing a hardworking colleague, what choice would you make?
Emily frequently reminds Andy that 'a million girls would kill for this job'. Does the prestige of working at Runway ultimately justify the emotional toll it takes on assistants?
★ What factors determine whether a high-prestige, high-stress job is worth pursuing?
🟣 Cinema Spotlight : Scene Study & Adaptation▲

Compare the adaptation differences and visual contrasts in this film.

Novel vs. Screenplay Adaptations
• In Lauren Weisberger's novel, Miranda is purely monstrous without redeeming qualities; screenwriters added her vulnerable Paris hotel scene to humanize her.
• Andy's final confrontation in Paris is altered from an angry outburst in the book to a quiet, dignified departure in the film.
Visual Directing & Setting Contrasts
• High-contrast lighting and clean architectural glass in the Runway offices emphasize cold, sterile corporate perfection.
• Meryl Streep's deliberate whisper forces every actor-and the audience-to lean in, creating an atmosphere of absolute control.
🟢 Part 2 : Artistry, Interpretation & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Artistic Analysis
Analyze the cinematography, visual motifs, directorial choices, and narrative themes. Integrate Imperatives & Softened Directives into your responses.
Director David Frankel contrasts Andy's simple morning routine with fashion models meticulously choosing outfits. How does this visual opening establish the cultural clash at the heart of the film?
★ What visual details in movies best communicate a character's social status or lifestyle?
Meryl Streep famously chose to speak Miranda's lines in a quiet, measured whisper rather than yelling. How does this performance choice enhance Miranda's chilling presence?
★ Which acting choices in film do you find most effective at creating tension?
Andy's boyfriend Nate argues that Runway is transforming her into someone she originally looked down on. Do you view Andy's transformation as professional growth or moral decline (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ Can a person change their habits and appearance for a job without changing who they are deep down?
The Paris sequence serves as both the peak of Andy's career success and her moment of moral awakening. Why is throwing her phone into the fountain the necessary resolution to her journey (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ What symbol or action would represent a dramatic turning point in your own life decisions?
Nigel dedicates years of his life to Runway, only for Miranda to sacrifice his promotion to safeguard her own position. What does his tragic reaction reveal about corporate loyalty?
★ How can professionals protect themselves from giving total loyalty to an organization that may not return it?
Costume designer Patricia Field uses Andy's changing wardrobe-from mismatched skirts to tailored Chanel boots-to mirror her internal shift. Which outfit change best marks her transition?
★ How do your clothing choices reflect your mood, confidence, or professional role?
Emily's desperate attempts to diet and prepare for Paris culminate in her street accident. How does the hospital scene highlight her tragic reliance on Miranda's approval?
★ Why do people sometimes seek validation from leaders who treat them poorly?
How does the film balance satirizing the absurdities of the fashion industry with genuinely celebrating its artistry and hard work?
★ Can a film mock an industry while simultaneously honoring the dedication of the people within it?
In Paris, Miranda shows rare vulnerability while discussing her impending divorce, only to make a ruthless business decision hours later. What makes her such a compelling anti-hero?
★ Do you prefer movie antagonists who are purely evil, or those who display complex human flaws?
If you were directing the final scene outside the New York fashion show, would you have Miranda acknowledge Andy with a nod, or should their separation have been completely cold?
★ How important is subtle mutual respect between former rivals at the end of a story?
