🎬 Session Vocabulary

1. Generic Discussion Vocabulary

gender barriers
social or institutional rules that restrict opportunities based on a person's gender.
In early 20th-century Eastern Europe, rigid gender barriers prohibited women from studying sacred religious texts.
theological study
the intellectual and academic examination of religious beliefs, scriptures, and divine laws.
Yentl possesses an insatiable passion for theological study, spending hours reading Talmud scriptures with her father.
internal authenticity
the quality of being true to one's own character, values, and intellectual desires despite external pressure.
Disguising herself as a man allows Yentl to preserve her internal authenticity as a dedicated student.
intellectual devotion
deep commitment and enthusiasm toward learning, thought, and academic pursuit.
Yentl's intellectual devotion drives her to abandon her home and enter a yeshiva disguised as Anshel.
social convention
a traditional rule or expectation of behavior accepted by a particular society or culture.
By refusing to marry and conform to traditional womanhood, Yentl defies every social convention of her village.

2. Authentic Film Quotes & Slang

yeshiva
(noun) a traditional Jewish educational institution focused on the study of rabbinic texts and theology.
Disguised as a young man named Anshel, Yentl travels to a famous yeshiva in Bechev to study Talmud.
Talmud
(noun) the central text of Rabbinic Judaism, containing discussions on law, ethics, customs, and history.
Yentl and Avigdor spend long afternoons passionately debating complex Talmud passages in the study hall.
Anshel
(proper noun / alter ego) the male pseudonym adopted by Yentl to gain admission to male-only study halls.
Yentl adopts her deceased brother's name, Anshel, cutting her hair and wearing men's clothes to enter the academy.
Papa, can you hear me?
(famous song line / dialogue) an iconic emotional plea expressing love, spiritual connection, and the search for guidance.
Yentl sings "Papa, can you hear me?" in the night, seeking approval from her late father for her hidden life.
there are no answers, only questions
(philosophical thematic quote) a key concept reflecting the Talmudic tradition of open debate and perpetual learning.
Yentl embraces the idea that genuine scholarship means realizing there are no simple answers, only deeper questions.

🔵 Part 1 : Dialogue, Themes & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Screenplay Discussion
Deconstruct the character conversations and screenplay dynamics. Integrate Gerunds as Subjects of Theological Study into your analysis.
In early 20th-century Eastern Europe, rigid gender barriers prevented women from engaging in sacred religious study. How does Yentl's secret study with her father challenge these community norms?
★ How do educational restrictions in historical settings compare to modern access to learning?
Yentl finds profound joy in theological study, spending late nights reading Talmud scriptures. How does reading religious texts with her father shape her intellectual confidence?
★ What subject or passion would you pursue with total devotion if societal obstacles were removed?
To honor her late father and preserve her internal authenticity, Yentl cuts her hair and adopts men's clothing. What risks does she accept by stepping into the world as Anshel?
★ Have you ever had to hide a part of yourself or make a drastic change to pursue what you love?
Her relentless intellectual devotion drives her to travel to a famous yeshiva in Bechev. How does her excitement at entering the study hall reflect her passion for debate?
★ What environment makes you feel most intellectually stimulated and engaged?
By rejecting traditional domestic expectations, Yentl breaks every social convention of her shtetl. How do the villagers react to her disinterest in marriage?
★ Why do communities often push individuals toward traditional life paths, and how can one resist?
At the academy, Anshel spends hours debating complex talmud passages with Avigdor. How does their shared love of scholarship create a deep emotional connection between them?
★ Do you find that intellectual debate brings people closer together or creates tension?
Adopting the male persona of anshel allows Yentl to be treated as an equal scholar. How does living as Anshel complicate her growing romantic feelings for Avigdor?
★ How difficult is it to maintain a false identity when strong personal feelings are involved?
Yentl sings 'Papa, can you hear me?' while gazing at the night sky. How does this song express her search for spiritual guidance and approval from her late father?
★ Who do you turn to-in memory or in person-when facing life's hardest choices?
When Hadass accepts Anshel as a suitor, Yentl finds herself trapped in an impossible marriage proposal. How does her affection for Hadass differ from her connection with Avigdor?
★ How can empathy for another person complicate an already difficult secret?
The study hall master teaches that 'there are no answers, only questions'. How does this Talmudic philosophy guide Yentl's ultimate decision at the end of the film?
★ Do you agree that asking deep questions is more valuable than having quick, easy answers?
🟣 Cinema Spotlight : Scene Study & Adaptation▲

Compare the adaptation differences and visual contrasts in this film.

Short Story vs. Screenplay Adaptations
• Isaac Bashevis Singer's original story ends ambiguously with Anshel disappearing into obscurity; Streisand's screenplay gives Yentl a triumphant voyage to America.
• All musical numbers were designed as internal monologues so Yentl never breaks reality in front of other characters.
Visual Directing & Setting Contrasts
• Streisand used authentic Czech locations and hand-crafted period costumes to ground the story in historical shtetl reality.
• Deep shadows in the study halls contrast with wide, open landscapes during Yentl's journey, symbolizing intellectual freedom.
🟢 Part 2 : Artistry, Interpretation & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Artistic Analysis
Analyze the cinematography, visual motifs, directorial choices, and narrative themes. Integrate Gerunds as Subjects of Theological Study into your responses.
Barbra Streisand directed, co-wrote, produced, and starred in Yentl after a decade of studio resistance. How does her personal struggle to make the film mirror Yentl's fight for education?
★ How does knowing a director's personal background influence your appreciation of their movie?
The cinematography uses warm candlelit tones for private study sessions and stark natural light for public spaces. How do these visual choices reflect Yentl's inner world versus public reality (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ How does lighting in period films help establish mood and intimate storytelling?
Unlike traditional musical theater where songs are sung to other characters, all of Streisand's songs in Yentl are internal monologues. How does this directorial choice heighten emotional intimacy (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ Do you prefer musical numbers that happen aloud within the scene or internal musical thoughts?
Avigdor is drawn to Anshel's sharp mind long before learning her true gender. How does the film explore attraction based on intellectual compatibility rather than physical appearance?
★ Is intellectual connection a stronger foundation for love than instant physical attraction?
When Yentl finally reveals her identity to Avigdor in the forest, his immediate reaction is confusion and anger. Was his response understandable given the cultural context of his era?
★ How should we evaluate historical characters when they react with prejudice to shocking revelations?
Hadass is raised strictly for traditional domestic life, yet she shows immense gentleness and sweetness. How does the film avoid making her seem inferior to Yentl?
★ Why is it important for films to respect different lifestyle choices among female characters?
At the conclusion, Yentl chooses to board a ship to America rather than stay with Avigdor as his wife. Why is emigration the only path that allows her to remain a scholar?
★ When is leaving everything behind the only way to achieve personal freedom?
How does Michel Legrand's lush orchestral score elevate Yentl's spiritual and academic journey throughout the story?
★ What role does music play in expressing emotions that dialogue alone cannot convey?
In what ways does Yentl address feminist themes that remain relevant to modern audiences around the world (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ Which modern barriers to gender equality or education do you think still need attention today?
If Yentl had agreed to marry Avigdor and live secretly as a traditional wife in Poland, how would that ending have changed the core message of the film?
★ Should movie endings prioritize romantic happiness or individual self-fulfillment?

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I recommend you to see this film→I recommend that you see this filmUse 'recommend that + subjunctive/bare infinitive' or 'recommend doing'.
The reason is because she was afraid→The reason is that she was afraidAvoid 'the reason is because'; use 'the reason is that' for formal/style clarity.
Seldom I have seen such an ending→Seldom have I seen such an endingNegative adverbs like 'seldom' at the beginning of a clause trigger subject-auxiliary inversion.
