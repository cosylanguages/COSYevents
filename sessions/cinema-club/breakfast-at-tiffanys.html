🎬 Session Vocabulary

1. Generic Discussion Vocabulary

social climbing
the strategic attempt to gain access to a higher socioeconomic class through charm and networking.
Holly Golightly uses her wit and glamorous outfits for social climbing among New York's wealthy bachelor elite.
bohemian lifestyle
an unconventional, informal way of living free from traditional social rules and routines.
Paul Varjak lives a bohemian lifestyle as a struggling writer living in a brownstone apartment subsidized by a married patron.
superficial glamour
an attractive, stylish outward appearance that disguises internal unhappiness or instability.
Holly hides her chaotic rural past behind a facade of dark sunglasses, cigarette holders, and superficial glamour.
emotional detachment
a psychological coping strategy of avoiding deep feelings or personal vulnerability.
Holly practices emotional detachment, refusing to name her cat or put furniture in her apartment to avoid belonging to anyone.
urban isolation
the sense of loneliness experienced despite living in a densely populated metropolis.
Both Holly and Paul experience urban isolation in New York City, connecting late at night through their fire escape windows.

2. Authentic Film Quotes & Slang

the mean reds
(iconic character idiom) Holly's nickname for sudden, existential terror and overwhelming anxiety.
Holly explains that when she gets "the mean reds", taking a taxi to Tiffany's jewelry store is the only thing that makes her feel safe.
given name Lula Mae
(pivotal plot line) the hidden rural identity Holly fled before reinventing herself in Manhattan.
Doc Golightly travels from Texas to New York, begging his runaway teenage wife Lula Mae to return to her farm family.
powder room
(period euphemism) a polite 1960s code phrase used when asking wealthy dates for money or restroom breaks.
Holly casually asks her wealthy escorts for fifty dollars for the "powder room" to pay her apartment rent.
wild thing
(character nickname quote) Paul's gentle nickname for Holly, highlighting her untamed, independent spirit.
Paul tenderly tells Holly, "You call yourself a free spirit, a wild thing, and you're terrified somebody's gonna stick you in a cage."
Moon River
(classic song title / theme) Henry Mancini's Oscar-winning song performed by Holly on her fire escape.
Holly sits in her window wearing a sweatshirt and towel, strumming a guitar and singing "Moon River".

🟣 Cinema Spotlight : Scene Study & Adaptation▲

Compare the adaptation differences and visual contrasts in this film.

Novella vs. Screenplay Adaptations
• Truman Capote's 1958 novella featured a darker, bisexually fluid Holly who flees to South America at the end; the film creates a conventional romantic happy ending in New York.
• The character of Paul Varjak was expanded from a nameless, passive narrator into a primary romantic lead played by George Peppard.
Visual Directing & Fashion Styling
• Hubert de Givenchy designed Holly's iconic black satin gown, paired with oversized dark sunglasses and pearl necklaces.
• Wide-angle cinematography captures the sparse, half-unpacked furniture inside Holly's apartment, reflecting her transient lifestyle.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I'm used to watch movies in original→I'm used to watching movies in originalThe phrase 'be used to' is followed by a gerund (-ing form), not a bare infinitive.
The reason is because she was afraid→The reason is that she was afraidAvoid 'the reason is because'; use 'the reason is that' for formal/style clarity.
Seldom I have seen such an ending→Seldom have I seen such an endingNegative adverbs like 'seldom' at the beginning of a clause trigger subject-auxiliary inversion.
