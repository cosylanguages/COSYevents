🎬 Session Vocabulary

1. Generic Discussion Vocabulary

mistaken identity
a situation where a person is wrongly believed to be someone else, leading to confusion or danger.
Reggie Lampert is bewildered by Peter Joshua's constant changing of names and mistaken identity throughout Paris.
wartime heist
a theft or robbery of military or government funds committed during an armed conflict.
Reggie discovers her late husband stole a quarter-million dollars in gold during a WWII OSS wartime heist.
shadowy conspiracy
a secretive plot conducted by a group of ruthless individuals seeking hidden money.
Three surviving military veterans form a shadowy conspiracy to interrogate Reggie and reclaim their stolen fortune.
chic suspense
a stylish blend of sophisticated visual glamour, witty dialogue, and high-stakes thriller tension.
Director Stanley Donen creates a atmosphere of chic suspense against the backdrop of 1960s Parisian cafes and fashion.
duplicitous charm
using attractive, polite behavior to conceal deceitful or dishonest intentions.
Cary Grant's character uses duplicitous charm to gain Reggie's trust while hiding his true government objective.

2. Authentic Film Quotes & Slang

any number can play
(famous line / dialogue quote) Cary Grant's witty response when Reggie warns him about dangerous men pursuing her.
When Reggie warns that three killers are hunting her, Peter dryly remarks "Any number can play!".
stolen fortune
(central mystery motif) the missing two hundred and fifty thousand dollars converted into rare postage stamps.
The entire cast scrambles across Paris searching for the stolen fortune, unaware it was used to buy three valuable stamps.
Givenchy wardrobe
(fashion motif) the elegant custom haute couture outfits designed by Hubert de Givenchy for Audrey Hepburn.
Audrey Hepburn wanders through Parisian thriller set-pieces wearing a flawless Givenchy wardrobe.
don't bite, I'm harmless
(playful dialogue quote) Peter's ironic reassurances to Reggie during their romantic banter.
Peter attempts to reassure the suspicious widow by joking, "Do you know what's wrong with you? Nothing. Don't bite, I'm harmless."
Paris stamps
(pivotal plot twist) the ordinary-looking vintage stamps glued to an envelope containing the entire fortune.
Reggie realizes the priceless stamps were sitting on an open market table after giving them to a young stamp collector.

🔵 Part 1 : Dialogue, Themes & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Screenplay Discussion
Deconstruct the character conversations and screenplay dynamics. Integrate Conditionals of Suspense & False Identities into your analysis.
Reggie Lampert returns to Paris to find her apartment stripped bare and her husband murdered, sparking a wild case of mistaken identity. How does she navigate the confusion?
★ How would you react if you discovered a close relative led a completely secret double life?
She learns her late husband stole a fortune during a WWII OSS wartime heist. How does the mystery of the missing two hundred and fifty thousand dollars drive the plot?
★ Why do missing fortune mysteries make such durable foundations for classic suspense cinema?
Three ruthless wartime veterans form a shadowy conspiracy to corner Reggie in Paris. How does she determine which of them poses the greatest threat?
★ How do you maintain composure when you feel surrounded by people you cannot trust?
Director Stanley Donen crafts an atmosphere of chic suspense, combining Givenchy fashion with sudden dark violence. How does style elevate the tension?
★ Can stylish glamour and genuine thriller suspense co-exist effectively in modern cinema?
Cary Grant's character changes his name four times, using duplicitous charm to keep Reggie guessing his true identity. Why does she keep trusting him?
★ What makes a charming person hard to resist even when you suspect they are hiding the truth?
When Reggie warns Peter about the dangerous men hunting her, he dryly quips 'any number can play'. How does his witty banter lighten the threat?
★ How can dry humor and witty dialogue diffuse panic during high-stakes situations?
The entire cast hunts ruthlessly for the stolen fortune, unaware of where Charles hid the funds. How does the screenplay keep the audience guessing (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ Do you enjoy thrillers where the audience solves the mystery alongside the main character?
Audrey Hepburn moves through dramatic set-pieces wearing a flawless givenchy wardrobe. How does her elegant look contrast with the grit of metro stations and dark alleys?
★ How does costume design influence the mood and visual sophistication of a mystery film?
Peter reassures the frantic widow by joking, 'don't bite, I'm harmless'. How does their romantic banter build romantic chemistry amid danger?
★ What role does playful banter play in building romantic connection between two sharp characters?
The mystery resolves when Reggie discovers that three rare Paris stamps on an envelope hold the entire fortune. How does this clever twist reframe earlier scenes?
★ What is your favorite clever plot twist or hidden revelation in mystery cinema history?
🟣 Cinema Spotlight : Scene Study & Adaptation▲

Compare the adaptation differences and visual contrasts in this film.

Original Screenplay & Genre Mashup
• Screenwriter Peter Stone originally wrote the story as a screenplay, converted it to a novel when studios rejected it, then sold the movie rights after it became a book.
• Masterfully blends three distinct genres: romantic comedy, Parisian travelogue, and Hitchcockian murder mystery.
Visual Directing & Setting Contrasts
• Features high-fashion Givenchy costuming shot against gritty Parisian metro stations, rooftops, and dark theater basements.
• Dynamic lighting in the climax uses stage trapdoors and spotlight beams to create dramatic silhouettes during the final chase.
🟢 Part 2 : Artistry, Interpretation & Grammar (10 Items)▲
Artistic Analysis
Analyze the cinematography, visual motifs, directorial choices, and narrative themes. Integrate Conditionals of Suspense & False Identities into your responses.
Director Stanley Donen is often called 'the master of the hitchcockian thriller not directed by Hitchcock'. How does Charade mirror Hitchcock's style while maintaining its own comedic lightness?
★ What directorial techniques distinguish a suspense thriller from a romantic comedy?
Audrey Hepburn and Cary Grant shared a 25-year age gap, leading Grant to request that Hepburn's character pursue him romantically. How did this choice affect the humor?
★ How can clever script adjustments make romantic pairings with age differences feel playful and appropriate?
Henry Mancini's moody, jazzy score features an iconic title theme with Latin percussion. How does the soundtrack reinforce the film's Parisian sophistication (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ How does sophisticated jazz music contribute to the atmosphere of 1960s suspense cinema?
The climax inside an empty Parisian theater, with trapdoors and stage lifts, provides a thrilling visual setting for the final showdown. How effective was this set-piece?
★ What makes old theaters and stage settings inherently dramatic locations for thriller climaxes?
Walter Matthau delivers a memorable supporting performance as CIA agent Hamilton Bartholomew, who turns out to be the real villain. Were you surprised by his twist?
★ What visual clues or performance choices make a secondary character twist surprising yet logical?
The funeral scene, where the three villains test Charles's casket with a pin and a mirror, blends macabre dark comedy with mystery. How does humor function in thriller openings?
★ Do you enjoy movies that mix dark, macabre humor with serious crime investigation?
How does the movie use famous Parisian locations-the Seine riverboats, Palais-Royal gardens, and stamp markets-as active narrative elements (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ How can a movie turn a real-world city into a living character within the story?
Reggie's ultimate decision to surrender the valuable stamps to the US Treasury showcases her moral integrity. How does her honesty contrast with the greed of the villains (e.g., examining light, framing, or pacing choices)?
★ Why is it satisfying when a protagonist chooses ethics over keeping a life-changing fortune?
The final scene reveals Peter's true identity as Brian Cruikshank, a Treasury official. How does this happy reveal resolve their romantic tension?
★ What makes a romantic comedy-thriller ending leave the audience smiling?
If you were remaking Charade for a contemporary audience, who would you cast in the lead roles originally played by Audrey Hepburn and Cary Grant?
★ Which modern movie stars possess the comedic timing, elegance, and charm needed for a sophisticated mystery remake?

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Seldom I have seen such an ending→Seldom have I seen such an endingNegative adverbs like 'seldom' at the beginning of a clause trigger subject-auxiliary inversion.
If they would have warned us, we would know→If they had warned us, we would knowUse past perfect (had + past participle) in the if-clause of a mixed conditional.
He insisted she goes to Runway→He insisted she go to RunwayVerbs of demand/insistence like 'insist' trigger the subjunctive/bare infinitive.
