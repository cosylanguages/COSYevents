🎬 Session Vocabulary

1. Generic Discussion Vocabulary

traditional manufacturing
the conventional process of producing physical goods using established factory labor and heritage methods.
Charlie Price inherits Price & Son, a traditional manufacturing factory struggling to survive in a modern footwear market.
drag culture
the performance art and community centered around expressive, gender-exaggerated theatrical costumes and shows.
Lola introduces Charlie to drag culture, explaining that performing drag queens require sturdy, specialized footwear.
niche market
a specialized, distinct segment of the market targeting a specific group of customers.
To save his family business from bankruptcy, Charlie pivots from plain menswear shoes to a niche market: high-heeled boots for drag queens.
cultural acceptance
the act of recognizing, respecting, and embracing individuals with different identities and lifestyles.
The factory workers gradually move from prejudice to cultural acceptance as they get to know Lola's warmth and strength.
unlikely partnership
a collaborative relationship formed between two people from drastically different backgrounds.
The unlikely partnership between a conservative English shoe maker and a flamboyant London drag queen revitalizes the factory.

2. Authentic Film Quotes & Slang

kinky boots
(title phrase / slang) provocative, eye-catching high-heeled footwear designed for dramatic performance and adult glamour.
Lola inspects Charlie's first boot design and insists that "kinky boots" must be made of vibrant red leather with reinforced steel heels.
sex is in the heel
(iconic dialogue quote) Lola's design philosophy explaining that a boot's silhouette and confidence come from its heel height.
Lola rejects Charlie's burgundy prototype, declaring "Sex is in the heel, darling! Red is the color of passion!"
change your mind about someone
(core thematic quote) the central moral message of the film regarding tolerance and empathy.
Lola delivers the film's key message during the Milan fashion show: "You change your mind about someone when you change your mind about yourself."
cobbler
(noun) a skilled craftsperson who repairs or manufactures shoes.
The factory workers take pride in their heritage as traditional cobblers, but must adapt their craft to build sturdy 6-inch heels.
drag queen
(noun) a performer, typically male, who adopts an exaggerated feminine persona and glamour for stage entertainment.
Lola commands the stage at the club as a top drag queen, commanding respect through her vocal talent and quick wit.

🟣 Cinema Spotlight : Scene Study & Adaptation▲

Compare the adaptation differences and visual contrasts in this film.

True Events vs. Screenplay Adaptations
• Based on the real W.J. Brooks factory in Northamptonshire, which saved itself by manufacturing specialized footwear called 'Divine'.
• Harvey Fierstein and Cyndi Lauper adapted this film into a smash Broadway musical, winning 6 Tony Awards including Best Musical.
Visual Directing & Setting Contrasts
• Industrial brick factories in Northampton painted in muted earth tones contrast sharply with the neon pinks and golds of London drag clubs.
• Camera work during the Milan runway sequence uses dynamic low angles to celebrate the height and power of the high-heeled boots.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
He insisted she goes to Runway→He insisted she go to RunwayVerbs of demand/insistence like 'insist' trigger the subjunctive/bare infinitive.
I'm used to watch movies in original→I'm used to watching movies in originalThe phrase 'be used to' is followed by a gerund (-ing form), not a bare infinitive.
Seldom I have seen such an ending→Seldom have I seen such an endingNegative adverbs like 'seldom' at the beginning of a clause trigger subject-auxiliary inversion.
