Quote

"Knowledge isn't free. You have to pay attention."

Richard Feynman is the person who unlocked the mysteries of quantum physics while passionately defending intellectual freedom, playfulness, and honesty, and in this session they focus on the following quote: "Knowledge isn't free. You have to pay attention." Why do we treat learning like a rigid, disciplined chore rather than a wild, original adventure? Is there a deeper wisdom in admitting we do not know everything? Join us as we dismantle formal educational dogmas and celebrate intellectual humility.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Inquisitiveness
having or showing an interest in learning things; curious.
His natural inquisitiveness drove him to take apart old watches to see how they worked.
Vigilance
the action or state of keeping careful watch for possible danger or difficulties.
We must maintain constant mental vigilance to avoid falling for misinformation online.
Complacency
a feeling of smug or uncritical satisfaction with oneself or one's achievements.
We cannot afford complacency when it comes to keeping our skills updated.
Cognitive
relating to the mental action or process of acquiring knowledge and understanding.
Sleep deprivation has a severe negative impact on our cognitive stamina.
Disruptive
causing or tending to cause disruption or unsettling focus.
Notifications are highly disruptive to deep study sessions.
Preserve
to maintain something in its original or existing state.
Reading physical books helps preserve our capacity for long-term concentration.
Rigor
the quality of being extremely thorough, exhaustive, or accurate.
Scientific research demands absolute academic rigor to verify hypotheses.
Assimilate
take in and fully understand information or ideas.
It takes years of practice to properly assimilate the nuances of a foreign language.
Cursory
hasty and therefore not thorough or detailed.
A cursory reading of the headline is never enough to understand the full article.
Indispensable
absolutely necessary; essential.
Active participation is indispensable if you want to become fluent in a language.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Inversion for Emphasis▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Upper-Intermediate (B2))

Use inversion for emphasis after negative or restrictive adverbials (rarely, seldom, little did I know, not only) to sound formal and emphatic.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
We must pay the attention→We must pay attention(Omit 'the' in the idiom 'pay attention')
I am study physics→I study physics(Simple present structure)
It depend on how much effort you do→It depends on how much effort you make(Collocation 'make an effort', singular verb)
