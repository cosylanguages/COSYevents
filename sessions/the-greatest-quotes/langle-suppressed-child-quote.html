Alfried Längle is the person who developed existential analysis to help individuals find inner consent and live authentic lives, and in this session they focus on the following quote: "If a child is always convenient, it means they have already suppressed a part of themselves." What is the true cost of being 'convenient' for everyone else? When we suppress our emotional needs to please others, how do we eventually find our way back to our authentic selves? Join us for a profound analysis of childhood and adult mental health.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Convenient
fitting in well with a person's needs, activities, and plans; causing little trouble or effort.
A quiet, quiet child is highly convenient for busy parents, but is it healthy for the child?
Suppression
the act of forcibly keeping something from happening, being expressed, or revealed.
The total suppression of a child's emotions can lead to serious psychological problems in adulthood.
Personality
the combination of characteristics or qualities that form an individual's distinctive character.
Every child deserves the space to develop a unique personality rather than conforming to rigid models.
Expression
the process of making known one's thoughts, opinions, or feelings.
Allowing open expression of anger or frustration is essential for a child's healthy growth.
Expectations
strong beliefs that something will happen or be the case in the future.
Children often suppress their true feelings to meet the heavy expectations of their parents.
Authentic
genuine, real, and true to one's own character, desires, and identity.
To live an authentic life, a person must learn to say 'no' even when it causes conflict.
Obey
to submit to the authority of or comply with the orders of someone.
Children who are forced to obey every single order without question might lose their sense of autonomy.
Boundaries
the limits or rules set to establish safe and respectful behavior in relationships.
Children need strong, consistent boundaries to feel secure while exploring their identity.
Freedom ≠ Captivity
the power or right to act, speak, or think as one wants without hindrance ≠ the state of being imprisoned or confined.
A child who lacks the freedom to make choices will struggle to build self-reliance, feeling as if they are in mental captivity.
Responsibility
the state or fact of having a duty to deal with something or having control over oneself.
Teaching children responsibility is far more effective than demanding strict, blind obedience.

Why do traditional educational systems reward kids who silently obey over those who demand creative expression?

🟣 Let's Speak Together : The Mask of Convenience 🎭▲

Discuss with your partner: Which form of childhood convenience is most likely to cause emotional struggles in adult life? Rank them from 1 (Most Damaging) to 4 (Least Damaging):

🤫 Profile A: The Silent Conformist
Never complains about chores or rules, always conforms, but hides their true personality and desires.
💼 Profile B: The Academic Achiever
Overworks constantly to meet parent expectations, suppresses play, and ties their self-worth strictly to grades.
😇 Profile C: The Emotional Caretaker
Always suppresses their own worries to keep their parents happy, serving as a convenient family peacekeeper.
🤐 Profile D: The Obedient Listener
Obeys every single command instantly without question, completely sacrificing personal freedom and choices.

Use modal verbs (should, must, ought to) and vocabulary words to justify your ranking!

🏁 Closing Circle: Complete the Sentence▲

Finish the session by going in a circle. Each student must complete the following sentence with exactly one adjective or short phrase using the vocabulary learned:

"An always convenient child is..."

Examples: suppressing themselves, authentic but hidden, quiet but struggling, meeting expectations, obeying without freedom.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
He have went to Paris → He has gone to Paris (Present perfect third-person singular 'has' + past participle 'gone'.)
We discussed about the problem → We discussed the problem / had a discussion about the problem ('Discuss' is transitive and takes no preposition, or use 'have a discussion about'.)
I am agree with you → I agree with you (Use 'agree' directly as a verb rather than using it with the auxiliary 'am'.)
