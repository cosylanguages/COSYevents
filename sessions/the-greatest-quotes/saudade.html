Topic

Emotional Depth and Untranslatable Words

Portuguese Poets is the person who represents the collective, quiet wisdom of observers who find deep meaning in the fabric of everyday life, and in this session they focus on the following quote: "Saudade is a deep emotional state of nostalgic longing for an absent something or someone." How do we find a sense of place and belonging in a fast-paced, shifting world? Is home a geographic space, or is it a specific time frozen in our memory? Let us explore how we perceive time, direction, and the lives of those around us.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Longing
a strong wish for something or someone.
He felt a deep longing to return to his childhood home.
Melancholy
a feeling of pensive sadness, typically with no obvious cause.
The rainy afternoon filled her with a sense of gentle melancholy.
Untranslatable
not able to be expressed in another language.
Many Portuguese speakers believe that 'saudade' is completely untranslatable.
La nostalgia ≠ La modernidad
sentimiento de pena por la lejanía o el tiempo pasado ≠ cualidad de lo moderno o actual.
Sintió una profunda nostalgia por el pueblo antiguo, un contraste total con la fría modernidad.
Wistful
having or showing a feeling of vague or regretful longing.
She gave a wistful smile as she remembered her days at university.
Evocative
bringing strong images, memories, or feelings to mind.
The smell of lavender was highly evocative of her grandmother's garden.
Bittersweet
arousing pleasure tinged with sadness or pain.
It was a bittersweet moment when they said goodbye at the airport.
Poignant
evoking a keen sense of sadness or regret.
The film's ending was incredibly poignant and left the audience in tears.
Ephemeral
lasting for a very short time.
The beauty of the sunset is ephemeral, fading within minutes.
Resonance
the quality in a sound or image of being deep, full, and reverberating.
His words had a deep emotional resonance with everyone in the room.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Habitual Past (Used to vs. Would)▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Advanced (C1))

Contrast bygone memories, childhood, and feelings of nostalgia using used to (for past habits or states) and would (only for past repeated actions).

📝 Task 1 : Part A: Rule Formulation:

Complete the rule: We use _____ for both past states and active habits that are no longer true. We use _____ only for repeated actions in the past, but never for past _____.

📝 Task 1 : Part B: Structured Practice:
  1. We _____ that home was a safe geographical space.
  2. Every winter, my grandmother _____ us old nostalgic letters.
  3. I _____ a very different perspective on what makes me happy.
  4. They _____ along the quiet riverbanks for hours.
  5. She _____ very attached to physical places from her childhood.
🗣️ Task 2 : Interactive Speaking:

Describe what you used to do when you felt nostalgic as a child. How would your family spend quiet evenings?

It is depend of the person→It depends on the person(Structure and preposition)
I am agree with you→I agree with you(Verb agreement)
It is depend on you→It depends on you(Verb vs Adjective)
