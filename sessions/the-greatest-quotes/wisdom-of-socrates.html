Socrates is the person who challenged the foundation of Western thought by asking questions that exposed the limits of human certainty, and in this session they focus on the following quote: "The only true wisdom is in knowing you know nothing." Why are we so terrified of admitting we know nothing? Does our modern obsession with having an opinion on everything prevent us from acquiring true, quiet wisdom? Join us for a foundational debate on intellectual humility.

☕ The Philosophers' Ledger: Café Debates

"The only true wisdom is in knowing you know nothing." : Socrates

📖 Session Vocabulary

Intellectual humility
a conscious awareness of the limitations of one's own knowledge.
Embracing intellectual humility is the first step toward genuine learning.
Preconceptions
opinions or ideas formed beforehand without adequate evidence or experience.
Socratic questioning forces us to strip away our comfortable preconceptions.
Ignorance
lack of knowledge, information, or awareness.
Socrates argued that recognizing your own ignorance is the beginning of true wisdom.
Dogmatism
the arrogant assertion of opinions as truths without proof or open-mindedness.
Socrates challenged the dogmatism of local politicians who pretended to know everything.
Paradox
a seemingly absurd or self-contradictory statement that actually reveals a deep truth.
The Socratic paradox-that we know nothing-proves that real wisdom is self-awareness.
Inquiry
a systematic investigation or the act of asking deep, searching questions.
Through relentless inquiry, Socrates exposed the shallow arguments of his peers.
Dialogue
a collaborative conversation aimed at uncovering truth rather than winning an argument.
A Socratic dialogue requires active listening and a shared pursuit of clarity.
Virtue
moral excellence, goodness, or the lived expression of high ethical standards.
In ancient Athens, Socrates debated whether virtue could be taught or if it was innate.
Cognizant
fully aware of or possessing conscious knowledge of something.
Being cognizant of our own cognitive biases prevents us from making foolish assumptions.
Contemplate
to think deeply and thoughtfully about philosophical or complex matters.
He would often stand in the public square to contemplate the nature of justice.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Inversion for Emphasis▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Advanced (C1))

Use inversion for emphasis after negative or restrictive adverbials (rarely, seldom, little did I know, not only) to sound formal and emphatic.

🟣 Let's Speak Together : The Hierarchy of True Understanding 🧠▲

Discuss with your partner: Rank these types of minds from most to least wise, according to Socrates' philosophy:

🧘 The Silent Contemplator
Admits ignorance and quietly contemplates life's deep mysteries without arguing or lecturing others.
🔍 The Passionate Inquirer
Actively questions every social rule, authority, and traditional custom, seeking true, objective virtue.
🎓 The Expert Specialist
Possesses vast technical knowledge but remains completely dogmatic and narrow-minded outside their field.
🤝 The Peaceful Diplomat
Prefers harmonious dialogue and polite compromise over challenging others' preconceptions.

Use inversion for emphasis (e.g., only if we challenge dogmatism can we..., rarely do experts...) to justify your hierarchy!

🏁 Closing Circle: Complete the Sentence▲

Finish the session by going in a circle. Each student must complete the following sentence with exactly one adjective or short phrase using the vocabulary learned:

"True wisdom is..."

Examples: admitting our own ignorance, letting go of dogmatism, cultivating intellectual humility, a continuous journey of inquiry, stripping away preconceptions.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
I am more wise→I am wiser(Short adjectives use comparative suffix '-er')
Socrates was an expert of questioning→Socrates was an expert at questioning(Use preposition 'at' after 'expert')
The most of people think...→Most people think...(Use 'Most people' for general statements)
If I was him, I would...→If I were him, I would...(Subjunctive mood in conditional structures)
I am agree with you→I agree with you(Agree is a verb and does not take the auxiliary 'am')
