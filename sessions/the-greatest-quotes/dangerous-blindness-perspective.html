Dangerous Blindness: The Limit of Perspective

Topic

Cognitive Biases and Narrow Perspectives

Perspective Advocates is the person who represents the collective, quiet wisdom of observers who find deep meaning in the fabric of everyday life, and in this session they focus on the following quote: "The most dangerous blindness is to believe that one's own perspective is the only possible reality." How do we find a sense of place and belonging in a fast-paced, shifting world? Is home a geographic space, or is it a specific time frozen in our memory? Let us explore how we perceive time, direction, and the lives of those around us.

☕ The Philosophers' Ledger: Café Debates

"Let us see how many interpretations exist! There is never only one correct answer to any profound human question." : Oscar Wilde

📖 Session Vocabulary

Perspective
a particular attitude toward or way of regarding something.
Traveling abroad gives you a completely new perspective on your own culture.
Blindness
lack of perception, awareness, or judgment.
Intellectual blindness prevents us from seeing alternative viewpoints.
Bias
prejudice in favor of or against one thing, person, or group.
Cognitive bias often distorts our logical decision-making.
Reinforce
strengthen or support (an existing feeling, idea, or habit).
Echo chambers on social media tend to reinforce our existing beliefs.
Insular
ignorant of or uninterested in cultures, ideas, or peoples outside one's own.
Living in an insular community can limit your intellectual growth.
Fallacy
a mistaken belief, especially one based on unsound argument.
His logical argument was actually built on a dangerous fallacy.
Echo chamber
an environment where a person only encounters information that reflects their opinions.
Social media algorithms construct an insular echo chamber.
Intellect
the faculty of reasoning and understanding objectively.
A sharp intellect requires the humility to admit mistakes.
Dogma
a principle or set of principles laid down by an authority as incontrovertibly true.
Healthy skepticism helps us question established religious or social dogma.
Empathy
the ability to understand and share the feelings of another.
Active listening is crucial for building deep empathy with opponents.

🎙️ Discussion Structure

🟠 Warm-up▲
Think about a time you were completely wrong about something.
  • Why is it so hard to admit that we have "blind spots"?
  • Do you usually seek out information that proves you are right or information that challenges you?
⚡ Grammar Practice : Passive Voice with Reporting Verbs▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Advanced (C1))

Use passive voice with reporting verbs (it is said, is believed to be, is thought that) to discuss public opinions, social conditioning, and rumors objectively.

📝 Task 1 : Part A: Rule Formulation:

Complete the rule: When using reporting verbs in the passive, we can use 'It is + _____ + that clause' or 'Subject + is/are + past participle + _____'.

📝 Task 1 : Part B: Structured Practice:
  1. It is _____ that propaganda can easily alter public perception.
  2. The media is often _____ to manipulate societal values.
  3. Many people are _____ to accept opinions without questioning them.
  4. It is _____ that social media algorithms reinforce biases.
  5. Human cognitive biases are _____ to hinder independent logic.
🗣️ Task 2 : Interactive Speaking:

Is most of what we believe to be true just reported to us by others? How can we separate facts from beliefs?

★ How do you plan to protect your independent thinking in the next ten years?

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
It is a blindness spot→It is a blind spot(Idiomatic expression)
He is bias toward them→He is biased toward them(Noun vs Adjective)
I am live in an echo chamber since years→I have lived in an echo chamber for years / I've been living in an echo chamber for years(Present Perfect + Duration)
I am agree with you→I agree with you(Verb agreement)
It is depend on you→It depends on you(Verb vs Adjective)
