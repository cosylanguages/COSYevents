Françoise Dolto is the person who revolutionized child psychoanalysis by treating children as full human beings with their own rights and voices, and in this session they focus on the following quote: "A child has the right to be difficult, that is the only way they learn to be themselves." Why do we demand perfect convenience from children when resistance is the only way they learn to be themselves? Are we raising obedient followers, or are we brave enough to nurture independent thinkers? Let us discuss the fine line between loving guidance and restrictive conformity.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Independent
capable of thinking or acting on one's own without constant supervision.
Encouraging children to make their own choices builds a highly independent mind.
Boundaries
the limits or rules set to establish safe and respectful behavior in relationships.
Setting clear parental boundaries helps children feel secure while learning self-control.
Confident
feeling sure of one's own abilities, qualities, or ideas.
A child who is allowed to disagree with their parents often grows into a highly confident adult.
Respectful
showing politeness, care, and honor toward others and their rules.
Children can learn to be respectful of others while still expressing their own opinions.
Discipline
the practice of training people to obey rules or code of behavior.
Traditional discipline often focused strictly on obedience rather than understanding.
Stubborn
refusing to change one's mind or behavior despite argument or persuasion.
His stubborn resistance to bedtime rules was actually a normal step in self-discovery.
Curious
having a strong desire to learn, explore, or know about things.
A highly curious child will constantly ask 'why' and test the limits of rules.
Obedient
willing to comply with orders, rules, or requests from authority figures.
While an obedient child is easy to manage, they might struggle to make independent choices later.
Freedom
the power or right to act, speak, or think as one wants without hindrance.
Giving children the freedom to make mistakes is essential for building confidence.
Responsibility
the state or fact of having a duty to deal with something or of having control over oneself.
With greater personal freedom comes the need to learn personal responsibility.

How should parents balance the need for structure and boundaries with the child's natural demand for freedom and autonomy?

Can a child build genuine independent thinking without sometimes disappointing or resisting adults?

Is it possible for parents to set healthy boundaries without demanding strict, unconditional obedient behavior?
★ Did your parents let you disagree with them openly, or did they expect you to be quiet and obedient?
How does having a confident voice as a child affect a person's future sense of responsibility?
★ What rule or parental discipline from your childhood are you most grateful for today?
Why are curious children often labeled as difficult or problematic in traditional classrooms?
★ How much freedom did your parents give you to explore your own hobbies and interests?
How can parents remain respectful of a child's unique personality when enforcing daily discipline?
★ Have you ever had to choose between showing respect to authority and staying true to your own freedom?
Does a child need absolute freedom of self-expression to develop into a highly independent adult?
★ Do you think modern children have too much freedom compared to your own childhood?
How should parents react when a child's natural curious nature clashes with school rules and discipline?

🟣 Let's Speak Together : The Happiest Adult Profile 🏆▲

Which child profile below is most likely to develop into the happiest, most successful adult? Rank them and explain your reasoning to your partner:

😇 Profile A: The Compliant Learner
Always obeyed rules, never argued, and stayed quiet to avoid any conflict with authority.
🔥 Profile B: The Stubborn Rebel
Often questioned rules, made many mistakes, but always stood up for their personal freedom.
🧠 Profile C: The Curious Explorer
Highly stubborn, deeply curious about everything, occasionally crossed parental boundaries.
💼 Profile D: The Disciplined Thinker
Showed strong self-discipline, stayed respectful of rules, but struggled to express feelings.

Use the learned modal verbs (should, must) and vocabulary words to justify your choice!

🏁 Closing Circle: Complete the Sentence▲

Finish the session by going in a circle. Each student must complete the following sentence with exactly one adjective or short phrase using the vocabulary learned:

"A difficult child is..."

Examples: curious, independent, exhausting, brave, misunderstood, creative, honest.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
He have went to Paris → He has gone to Paris (Present perfect third-person singular 'has' + past participle 'gone'.)
We discussed about the problem → We discussed the problem / had a discussion about the problem ('Discuss' is transitive and takes no preposition, or use 'have a discussion about'.)
I am agree with you → I agree with you (Use 'agree' directly as a verb rather than using it with the auxiliary 'am'.)
