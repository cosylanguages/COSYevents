An Elementary (A2) science discussion on how sitting up straight improves posture, back health, mental focus, and daily mood.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Sit ≠ stand
to rest your body on a chair.
Try to sit up straight while reading.
Straight ≠ crooked
in a direct line; not curved or bent.
A straight back helps you breathe better.
Mood ≠ habit
the way you feel at a particular time.
A walk in the sun can improve your mood.
Back ≠ stomach
the rear part of the human body from the neck to the hips.
Sitting slouched causes pain in your lower back.
Chair ≠ floor
a seat for one person, typically with four legs and a back.
Choose a comfortable chair for your desk.
Energy ≠ fatigue
the strength required for sustained physical or mental activity.
Sitting straight gives you more daily energy.
Focus ≠ distraction
the center of interest or activity.
Good posture helps students focus on lessons.
Body ≠ mind
the physical structure of a human.
Your body language shows how you feel inside.
Health ≠ illness
the state of being free from illness or injury.
Daily stretches are good for back health.
Relax ≠ stress
to become less tense and more calm.
Take a deep breath to relax your shoulders.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
You should to sit straight. → You should sit straight. (Modal 'should' is followed by base verb without 'to'.)
I feel my back hurts. → My back hurts. (Express physical sensation directly: 'My back hurts'.)
