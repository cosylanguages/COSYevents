Based on ScienceDaily research (August 2026). Discover the specific brain cells and neural circuits that regulate drive, perseverance, dopamine release, and goal execution.

📖 Session Vocabulary

Motivation ≠ apathy
the reason or desires that compel someone to act.
Scientists discovered brain cells that control daily motivation.
Neuron ≠ blood cell
a specialized cell transmitting nerve impulses; a nerve cell.
Specific neural circuits fire when we initiate a tough task.
Circuit ≠ fragment
a neural pathway in the brain.
Dopamine circuits regulate feelings of reward and drive.
Perseverance ≠ surrender
persistence in doing something despite difficulty.
Brain cells dedicated to perseverance help us finish long projects.
Dopamine ≠ cortisol
a neurotransmitter associated with pleasure and motivation.
Dopamine release encourages repeated goal-oriented behavior.
Discharge ≠ accumulation
the firing or release of electrical activity in neurons.
Neuronal discharge increases when a reward is near.
Incentive ≠ deterrent
a thing that motivates or encourages one to do something.
Clear incentives activate brain circuits for action.
Fatigue ≠ vigor
extreme tiredness resulting from mental or physical exertion.
Overcoming mental fatigue depends on specific cell signaling.
Stimulus ≠ inertia
a thing or event that evokes a specific functional reaction.
A positive stimulus triggers neural drive to achieve goals.
Initiative ≠ passivity
the ability to assess and initiate things independently.
Taking initiative requires strong neural activation.

Use relative clauses to describe scientific discoveries (e.g., Scientists discovered brain cells which regulate motivation).

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
The cells who control motivation. → The cells which / that control motivation. (Use 'which' or 'that' for non-human objects and cells, not 'who'.)
Dopamine make us feels good. → Dopamine makes us feel good. (Causative verb 'make' takes base verb without '-s': 'makes us feel'.)
