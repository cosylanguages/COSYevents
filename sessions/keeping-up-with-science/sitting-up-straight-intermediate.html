📖 Session Vocabulary

Posture ≠ slouching
the position in which someone holds their body when standing or sitting.
Good posture improves lung capacity and alertness.
Decision-making ≠ hesitation
the process of making choices, especially important ones.
Sitting up straight boosts confidence in decision-making.
Cognitive ≠ physical
relating to the mental processes of perception and judgment.
Proper alignment reduces cognitive fatigue during long study sessions.
Upright ≠ slouched
vertical or erect in position.
Maintaining an upright posture signals self-assurance.
Mood ≠ apathy
a temporary state of mind or feeling.
Slouching is linked to lower mood and fatigue.
Alertness ≠ drowsiness
the state of being watchful and prompt to meet danger or emergency.
Upright seating enhances mental alertness.
Alignment ≠ distortion
arrangement in a straight line or in correct relative positions.
Spinal alignment supports optimal blood flow to the brain.
Ergonomic ≠ awkward
designed for efficiency and comfort in the working environment.
Ergonomic chairs encourage healthy daily seating habits.
Confidence ≠ self-doubt
the feeling or belief that one can rely on someone or something.
Adopting an upright posture instantly increases self-confidence.
Mechanism ≠ chaos
a system of parts working together in a machine or process.
The physiological mechanism involves hormone regulation and oxygen intake.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Good posture may to improve mood. → Good posture may improve mood. (Modal verb 'may' is followed by base verb without 'to'.)
I sit slouchedly at my desk. → I sit slouched at my desk. (Use participle adjective 'slouched' to describe seating posture.)
