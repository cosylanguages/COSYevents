📖 Session Vocabulary

Circuitry ≠ isolation
the network of interconnected neurons executing specific functions.
Complex neural circuitry dictates the threshold between perseverance and resignation.
Apathy ≠ drive
lack of interest, enthusiasm, or concern.
Identifying key motivational neurons offers hope for treating clinical apathy.
Neurotransmitter ≠ hormone
a chemical substance released at the end of a nerve fiber.
Dopamine functions as a primary neurotransmitter in reward pathways.
Modulation ≠ fixed state
the exertion of a modifying or controlling influence on something.
Targeted neural modulation could potentially alter behavioral persistence.
Volition ≠ compulsion
the faculty or power of using one's will.
Human volition relies on sophisticated prefrontal cortex signaling.
Endurance ≠ surrender
the capacity of something to last or withstand wear and tear.
Subcortical structures modulate physical and mental endurance under pressure.
Refractory ≠ responsive
stubborn or unmanageable; resistant to stimulus.
Overstimulated neural circuits may enter a refractory period of diminished drive.
Homeostasis ≠ imbalance
the tendency toward a relatively stable equilibrium.
Motivational drive operates within a tight homeostatic biological boundary.
Threshold ≠ sub-level
the magnitude or intensity that must be exceeded for a reaction to occur.
High-stress tasks raise the neural threshold required to initiate action.
Attenuation ≠ reinforcement
the reduction of the force, effect, or value of something.
Chronic stress leads to the attenuation of goal-oriented signaling.

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Advanced (C1))

Use inversion (Negative adverb + auxiliary + subject + verb) for sophisticated scientific discourse (e.g., Not only do these neurons fire during goal setting, but they also sustain effort).

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
Not only these neurons fire, but they sustain drive. → Not only do these neurons fire, but they sustain drive. (Inversion rule: negative adverb 'not only' requires auxiliary verb before subject.)
The research have disproved previous ideas. → The research has disproved previous ideas. (Singular subject 'research' takes singular verb 'has').)
