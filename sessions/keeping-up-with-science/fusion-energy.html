🎙️ Audio Briefing Transcription / Science Digest ▼

Imagine unleashing the power of a star inside a laboratory-could nuclear fusion solve humanity's clean energy needs forever? Recreating solar reactions on Earth requires pushing scientific innovation and language to its absolute limit as scientists describe states of matter that defy imagination. In this session, we explore cutting-edge Tokamak technology, debate the economic future of clean energy, and practice hypothetical conditionals. Come share your vision of the future, debate big technological questions, and power up your English skills!

📖 Session Vocabulary

Fusion
the process of joining two or more things together to form a single entity.
Nuclear fusion powers the sun and other stars.
Fission
the action of dividing or splitting something into two or more parts.
Traditional nuclear power plants use fission to generate electricity.
Plasma
an ionized gas consisting of positive ions and free electrons.
In a fusion reactor, the fuel must be heated until it becomes plasma.
Sustainability
the ability to be maintained at a certain rate or level.
Fusion is seen as a key to long-term energy sustainability.
Breakthrough
a sudden, dramatic, and important discovery or development.
Scientists recently achieved a major breakthrough in net energy gain.
Isotopes
forms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
Deuterium and tritium are isotopes of hydrogen used in fusion.
Magnetic confinement
using magnetic fields to contain fusion fuel in the form of a plasma.
The tokamak design uses magnetic confinement to stabilize the reaction.
Net energy gain
when the energy generated is greater than the energy required to start the reaction.
Achieving net energy gain is the primary goal of commercial fusion research.
Abundance
a very large quantity of something.
Hydrogen is found in great abundance in the Earth's oceans.
Radioactive waste
hazardous waste that contains radioactive material.
One advantage of fusion is that it produces very little long-lived radioactive waste.

⚡ Grammar Practice : Future Passive Voice▲

⚡ Grammar Explanation (Upper-Intermediate (B2))

Use the future passive (will + be + past participle) to talk about events or actions that will be performed on a receiver in the future.

✏️ Teacher's Note (Linguistic Corrections)▲
The government should invest more→Governments should invest more(Generalizing with plurals)
I am looking forward to see it→I am looking forward to seeing it(Looking forward to + -ing)
It didn't happened yet→It hasn't happened yet(Present Perfect for 'yet')
Explain me the process→Explain the process to me(Direct/Indirect object)
I am agree with you→I agree with you(Verb agreement)
